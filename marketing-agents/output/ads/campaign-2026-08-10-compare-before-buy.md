# Campaign Creative Batch — Compare Before You Buy

**Status:** Launch-ready drafts; not published.  
**Destination:** `/compare`  
**Guardrail:** No unsupported “cheapest,” savings, real-time, or guaranteed-alert claims.

## Google Search variations

### 1. Price context

- Headlines: `Compare Ticket Prices` / `Check 3 Ticket Sources` / `Compare Before You Buy`
- Descriptions: `Compare Ticketmaster, SeatGeek and StubHub for the same event in one place.` / `Skip the tab marathon. Check the market side by side before choosing a listing.`

### 2. Speed

- Headlines: `Ticket Prices Side By Side` / `One Search, More Context` / `Shop Tickets Faster`
- Descriptions: `Start with one event search instead of opening every marketplace in separate tabs.` / `See available ticket options together, then decide which listing fits your plan.`

### 3. Trust / consumer advocate

- Headlines: `Check Before Checkout` / `A Ticket Price Is Context` / `Shop With A Ceiling`
- Descriptions: `Compare the same event before a headline price turns into a rushed decision.` / `Set your budget, inspect the listing details, and choose with more context.`

### 4. Urgency without FOMO

- Headlines: `Before You Buy Those Tickets` / `Pause The Ticket Panic` / `Compare This Event`
- Descriptions: `A countdown is not a buying strategy. Compare available options before you commit.` / `Make a quick side-by-side check part of your ticket routine.`

### 5. Feature utility

- Headlines: `Find Your Event And Compare` / `Track Ticket Options` / `Compare Sports And Concerts`
- Descriptions: `Search sports and concerts, compare ticket sources, and keep the event on your radar.` / `Use one place to organize the options before you head to checkout.`

## Meta variations

### 6. Comparison utility

**Primary text:** Ticket shopping should not require tab archaeology. Compare Ticketmaster, SeatGeek and StubHub in one place before you choose a listing.  
**Headline:** Compare Before You Buy  
**Description:** More market context.

### 7. Price ceiling

**Primary text:** Pick your ticket ceiling before the listing picks it for you. Compare the event, decide what fits, and leave the panic-buying to the group chat.  
**Headline:** Set Your Ticket Ceiling  
**Description:** Budget first. Checkout second.

### 8. Same-seat discipline

**Primary text:** A lower number is not automatically a better deal. Match the event, section, quantity, delivery terms, and total before you compare.  
**Headline:** Compare Equivalent Tickets  
**Description:** Same event. Better context.

## Display / social image concepts

### 9. Tab archaeology

- **Copy:** `One event. Three sources. Fewer tabs.`
- **Visual direction:** Clean split-screen mockup with three marketplace columns, a single highlighted event, and a neutral TicketScan comparison panel. No competitor logos unless approved for use.
- **CTA:** `Compare before checkout`

### 10. The final-number check

- **Copy:** `The headline price is only the opening argument.`
- **Visual direction:** Large ticket listing number crossed by a simple “check the total” annotation; secondary checklist for section, quantity, delivery, and fees. Use dark navy, warm off-white, and one ticket-orange accent.
- **CTA:** `Check the event`

## Setup and QA

- Use `utm_source=google` or `meta`, `utm_medium=paid_search` or `paid_social`, `utm_campaign=compare_before_buy_2026_08`, and variant-specific `utm_content`.
- Keep audience, destination, and offer constant while testing creative angle.
- Minimum read: 3 days or 100 clicks per variant.
- Primary event: `price_comparison_completed`; secondary: `signup_completed`, `watchlist_added`.
- Hold all claims about current prices, drops, savings, and alerts until product QA passes.
