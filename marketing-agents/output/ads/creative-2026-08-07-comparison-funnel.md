# Paid Ad Creative — 2026-08-07

Three testable variations for the comparison funnel. Claims are limited to the verified USP: compare Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, and StubHub in one place. No unsupported savings, price-drop, or social-proof number is used.

## Variation 1 — Comparison, evergreen

**Angle:** Put the spread in front of the buyer before checkout.

### Google Search

- Headline 1 (30 chars): `Compare Ticket Prices`
- Headline 2 (30 chars): `Three Ticket Sources`
- Headline 3 (30 chars): `See The Spread Before You Buy`
- Description 1 (90 chars): `Compare Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, and StubHub in one place before you commit.`
- Description 2 (90 chars): `Same event, different listings. Check the math first. Free comparison tool.`

### Meta

- Primary text (125 chars): `The same event can cost different money on different sites. Compare Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, and StubHub before checkout.`
- Headline (40 chars): `Compare Before You Buy`
- Description (30 chars): `See all 3 sources side by side.`

## Variation 2 — Concert shopper

### Meta

- Primary text (125 chars): `Concert tabs multiplying? Compare Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, and StubHub in one search, then decide with the actual spread.`

## Variation 3 — Data-minded buyer

**Angle:** Consumer advocacy without a fabricated savings promise.

### Meta

## Test notes

- Start with Variation 1 for generic comparison intent, then Variation 2 for concert-qualified audiences.
- Hold Variation 3 until conversion events and UTMs are verified; its value is measurement-sensitive.
- Use `/` as the fallback destination. Use `/compare` after anonymous-flow QA passes.
- Do not publish alert-led or “save $X” variants until price freshness, alert delivery, and a defensible savings baseline are restored.
