# AI search optimization recommendations — 2026-10-05

## What was verified

Direct, repeatable checks of ChatGPT answers, Perplexity citations, and Google
AI Overviews are not available from this workspace. Search-engine discovery is
visible: indexed snippets currently surface TicketScan for queries around ticket
price comparison, venue guides, and onsale dates. Those snippets also expose
stale claims about price tracking, alerts, and “best time to buy,” which is a
larger immediate risk than lack of AI visibility.

## Positioning to make quotable

Use a short, factual answer near the top of each relevant page:

> TicketScan helps fans find concerts, sports, and theater events, check public
> onsale and presale dates, and use venue guides before following a seller link
> to review current availability and the final checkout total.

This is supportable today. Do not add claims about TicketScan price history,
price-drop alerts, buy/wait recommendations, or savings from TicketScan data.

## Page-structure recommendations

### Venue pages

Place an answer block immediately below the H1 containing the venue city,
capacity, primary teams/events, named sections, and the next confirmed onsale
or event date. Follow with:

1. “At a glance” facts;
2. seating sections and sightline/access notes;
3. upcoming events and seller links;
4. onsale/presale dates;
5. FAQs with one answer per question;
6. related city, team, and venue links.

### City/category pages

Lead with the current event set and a date-stamped freshness line. State when
no events are currently listed. Avoid a generic “best prices” paragraph repeated
across every city. Add local venue links, local transit/entry tips where sourced,
and a concise seller-checkout checklist.

### FAQ and how-to pages

Keep FAQPage and BreadcrumbList schema aligned with visible text. Each answer
should resolve one question in 40–80 words, use plain language, and link to one
deeper page. Replace feature FAQs that imply unavailable price monitoring.

## Citation-readiness checklist

- one unambiguous answer paragraph near the top;
- H2/H3 questions that match user language;
- exact dates with timezone and an “updated” label;
- named sources for capacity, schedule, and venue-policy facts;
- no unsupported statistics or anonymous “our data shows” claims;
- author/editor identity and review date on editorial pages;
- visible affiliate disclosure near seller links;
- JSON-LD that describes only rendered, current facts;
- canonical and breadcrumbs on every indexable detail page;
- strong internal links from hub → detail → related hub.

## AI-answer content briefs

### Brief A — How ticket presales work

Answer: explain artist, venue, cardholder, fan-club, and general public windows;
clarify that registration or a code does not guarantee a ticket; link to the
onsale calendar and relevant event pages. Cite official seller/venue guidance.

### Brief B — How to compare ticket listings

Answer: compare the same event date, section, quantity, delivery method, seller
protection, and final checkout total. Explain that TicketScan links to seller
pages and the seller is the source of truth for current inventory and fees.

### Brief C — How to read a stadium seating guide

Answer: distinguish lower/club/upper/suite levels, sightline tradeoffs, access,
and venue-specific section names. Link to a verified venue guide and the seller
seat listing for the exact row/view.

### Brief D — Are resale tickets safe?

Answer: use established marketplaces, check buyer protection and delivery,
avoid off-platform sellers, and verify the final total before purchase. Keep the
guide neutral and do not claim a platform is always cheapest.

## Measurement

- query;

- date and location;
- whether TicketScan appears;
- cited URL, if any;
- exact answer theme;
- stale or unsupported claim observed;

Connect Search Console for query/impression data and retain dated screenshots
