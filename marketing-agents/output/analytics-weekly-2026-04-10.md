## Competitor Deep-Dive: StubHub (Week 2 Rotation)

### Product

**Features StubHub has that we don't:**
- Interactive seat maps with view-from-seat photos
- Fan Protect guarantee (refund if event cancelled)
- Mobile app (iOS + Android) with mobile ticket delivery
- International event coverage (50+ countries)
- Price filtering with "all-in" pricing toggle
- Seller marketplace (users can list/sell tickets)
- "FanConnect" social features

**Features we have that StubHub doesn't:**
- Multi-source price comparison (StubHub only shows their own listings)
- Price history tracking and trends (when it works)
- Buy/wait/hold recommendation engine
- Free tier with no fees for price tracking
- Venue-specific guides with local tips

**UX/UI differences:**
- StubHub: polished, mobile-first, fast search with autocomplete, heavy on social proof ("X people viewing this event")
- TicketScan: cleaner but sparser, comparison-focused, no social proof elements

**Pricing model:**
- StubHub: marketplace model with 15-25% buyer fees + seller fees
- TicketScan: free comparison tool, no transaction fees (affiliate model)

### Marketing

**SEO:**
- StubHub dominates "[event name] tickets" keywords with domain authority
- They rank for 10M+ keywords (Ahrefs estimate)
- Strong venue pages, city pages, and artist pages — similar structure to TicketScan but with vastly more content and backlinks
- **Gap for us:** StubHub doesn't optimize for "cheap [event] tickets" or "[event] ticket price tracker" — comparison and savings-oriented queries are underserved

**Content:**
- StubHub blog covers event previews, travel guides, and "best seats" guides
- Heavy investment in event-specific landing pages
- No price comparison or savings content (conflict of interest — they want you to buy at current price)

**Social:**
- Twitter: 750K+ followers, daily posts, event-day engagement
- Instagram: 500K+ followers, high-quality event photography, Stories for live events
- TikTok: Growing presence with fan-focused short-form content
- **Our gap:** We have <100 followers across platforms

**Paid:**
- Heavy Google Ads spend on "[event] tickets" keywords
- Retargeting across display network
- Sports sponsorships (arena naming rights partnerships)

**Email:**
- Signup prompts for price drop alerts on specific events
- Weekly digest of trending events
- Abandoned cart recovery (marketplace model)
- Event reminder emails (day-before)

### Positioning

- **Their value prop:** "World's largest ticket marketplace" — selection and guarantee
- **Their target audience:** Mainstream ticket buyers who want convenience and trust
- **Differentiation:** Scale, guarantee, seat maps, mobile experience

### Opportunities

1. **"Compare StubHub prices" keyword cluster** — users searching to validate StubHub pricing are our ideal audience. Content + SEO play.
2. **Transparency angle** — StubHub hides fees until checkout. TicketScan shows all-in pricing across sources. Strong positioning.
3. **Price tracking is our moat** — StubHub wants you to buy now. We help you buy at the right time. Fundamentally different value prop.
4. **"Is StubHub legit" / "StubHub fees" queries** — informational content that naturally leads to comparison.
5. **Comparison page spec:** Build `/compare/ticketscan-vs-stubhub` landing page emphasizing: multi-source comparison, no fees, price history, buy timing recommendations.

### Threats

1. **Brand trust gap** — StubHub has 20+ years of brand recognition and a buyer guarantee. We have none.
2. **Mobile gap** — No native app. StubHub's mobile experience is significantly better.
3. **Data depth** — StubHub has real transaction data for pricing. We rely on listed prices from APIs.
4. **If StubHub adds price comparison** — their scale would immediately outclass us. Our window of differentiation is limited.
5. **SEO authority** — Their domain authority (90+) vs ours means we can't compete on head terms. Must win on long-tail and comparison queries.

## Strategic Assessment

TicketScan is at an inflection point. The product concept is validated — users who find us sign up, and users who sign up immediately engage. The 100% signup-to-watchlist conversion rate is remarkable and proves product-market fit for the "track event prices" use case.

However, the platform currently cannot deliver on its core promise. Users add events to their watchlist and then... nothing happens. No price tracking, no alerts, no onboarding emails. Every user's experience ends at "add to watchlist."

**Priority stack for next week:**
1. Fix price tracking cron (unblocks the entire value proposition)
2. Fix drip campaign (unblocks user retention)
3. Connect GA4 API (unblocks marketing optimization)
4. Improve target price UX (unblocks price alerts)
5. Then — and only then — invest in acquisition

The worst thing we can do right now is drive more traffic to a product that can't deliver. Fix the engine first, then fuel it.
