## Key Insights

### 1. Activation finally failed — and it happened during our weakest traffic week
For 3+ months, 100% of signups activated within minutes. This week we got 1 signup and it didn't activate. The two events are likely related: low-volume weeks tend to over-represent low-intent signups (curious browsers, not buyers). When the funnel is running well, the curious ones are drowned out by the buyers. When the funnel is empty, they're all we see. **Implication: focus on intent quality before chasing volume.**

We called this out as possible last Friday. One week later it's confirmed: 0 adds in 7 straight days. The 3 signups and 8 watchlist adds on Apr 9-10 were real, but not repeatable. Whatever drove that spike (timing, search algorithm blip, social share?) isn't systematic.

We can't even measure this (no retention data), but the logic is inescapable: users activate, see nothing happen, don't come back. The 34 users with 1+ watchlist items are silently churning out.

## Competitor Deep-Dive: Vivid Seats (Week 3 Rotation)

### Product

**Features Vivid Seats has that we don't:**
- Vivid Seats Rewards program (earn a free ticket after 10 purchases)
- Mobile app (iOS + Android) with push notifications and mobile ticket delivery
- 100% Buyer Guarantee (refund or replacement for canceled events, fake tickets, etc.)
- Interactive seat maps with row-level precision
- "Skybox" branded fan experiences
- Concierge service for premium buyers
- Saved payment methods and one-click checkout
- Large team sponsorships (MLB, NCAA, PGA partnerships)
- Official reseller deals with sports teams (direct team partnerships)

**Features we have that Vivid Seats doesn't:**
- Multi-source price comparison (Vivid shows only Vivid listings)
- Price history tracking (when infrastructure is fixed)
- Buy/wait/hold recommendation engine
- Free tier with zero transaction friction
- Venue-specific guides beyond event listings
- No hidden fees in comparison (Vivid adds ~20-30% at checkout)

**UX/UI differences:**
- Vivid Seats: polished, mobile-first, heavy social proof ("X bought in last hour"), color-coded deal indicators, strong urgency cues ("Only 4 left!")
- TicketScan: cleaner but sparser, comparison-focused, no urgency/social proof elements

**Pricing model:**
- Vivid Seats: marketplace with ~15-25% buyer fees + 10% seller fees + Rewards bundled in
- TicketScan: free comparison, no transaction fees (affiliate attribution)

### Marketing

**SEO:**
- Vivid Seats ranks for ~5M+ keywords (below StubHub but significant)
- Strong on: "[event] tickets," "[team] tickets," "[venue] tickets"
- Heavy city + venue page investment (similar to our structure, but with higher DA and more backlinks)
- **Gap:** They don't rank well on "cheap [event] tickets" or comparison-style queries — same gap we saw with StubHub. Reinforces that comparison/savings queries are an underserved vertical.
- **Long-tail opening:** "is vivid seats legit," "vivid seats fees," "vivid seats vs stubhub" — informational queries that naturally funnel into comparison content.

**Content:**
- Vivid Seats blog is strong on event previews, "best seats" guides, tour announcements
- Heavy focus on team/league content (reflecting sports sponsorship dollars)
- No price comparison content (structural conflict)
- Invests in original video content for high-value events

**Social:**
- Twitter: 400K+ followers, heavy sports engagement, partnership-driven content
- Instagram: 300K+ followers, event photography, fan features
- TikTok: Growing, fan-reaction and deal-focused short-form
- **Our gap:** same as with StubHub — orders of magnitude smaller presence

**Paid:**
- Aggressive Google Ads spend on "[event] tickets" head terms
- Sports team sponsorships generate organic brand mentions
- Retargeting across display and YouTube
- Heavy in-app/mobile spend

**Email:**
- Event-specific price drop alerts (they have this — and they deliver it)
- Weekly newsletter with deals and new events
- Rewards program milestone emails
- Reliable, high-frequency cadence

### Positioning

- **Their value prop:** "100% Buyer Guarantee + Rewards" — safety, trust, loyalty points
- **Target audience:** Repeat ticket buyers who want a rewards structure and a guarantee
- **Differentiation:** Loyalty program is a genuinely unique moat in this category (no competitor matches it at scale)

### Opportunities

1. **"Vivid Seats fees" / "Is Vivid Seats legit" keyword cluster** — high-intent informational searches. Content should lead to comparison tools (us).
2. **"Vivid Seats vs StubHub" comparison queries** — we can insert ourselves as the "compare both + more" option. Long-tail SEO win.
3. **Fee transparency angle** — Vivid Seats' biggest user complaint is surprise fees at checkout. Our all-in pricing comparison is a direct answer to that pain.
4. **Price tracking moat** — same as StubHub: Vivid wants you to buy now with urgency cues. We help you buy at the right time. Fundamentally different value prop.
5. **No rewards program from us — and we shouldn't build one.** Our moat is transparency + comparison, not lock-in. Resist the pull to match their feature list.

### Threats

1. **Rewards lock-in.** Every ticket a user buys through Vivid is 10% toward a free ticket. That's a real switching cost we can't match.
2. **Brand trust.** Vivid Seats has decades of brand recognition and a buyer guarantee. We have neither.
3. **Mobile app gap.** Vivid's app is a full competitor moat for mobile-first buyers. We don't have a mobile app at all.
4. **Team sponsorships.** Fans of sponsored teams see Vivid branding in stadiums, social, and official team channels. This is a distribution channel we cannot replicate.
5. **If Vivid or StubHub adds multi-source comparison** — their scale would immediately outclass us.

## Strategic Assessment

Last week's assessment was: "TicketScan is at an inflection point. The product works, the plumbing doesn't." This week's update:

**The plumbing still doesn't work, and now the product is starting to show cracks too.**

The 100% signup-to-activation rate was the single healthy number in our data. This week it broke. dylanbaldy is one data point and shouldn't be over-weighted — but the context matters: we got 1 signup in 7 days, and that one user didn't activate. The funnel isn't just small; it's leaking at a new seam.

**The priority stack has not changed in 10 days:**

1. Fix price tracking cron (unblocks the entire value proposition)
2. Fix drip campaign (unblocks user retention)
3. Connect GA4 API (unblocks marketing optimization — including the ability to diagnose dylanbaldy-type failures)
4. Improve target price UX (unblocks price alerts — the feature users actually subscribed for)
5. Ship the 24h post-signup nudge email (cheapest possible retention hook)
6. Then — and only then — invest in acquisition

**One new strategic question to surface:** at what point does continued acquisition investment become net-negative for the brand? We haven't run paid yet, but organic traffic is finding us and the product isn't delivering. Every unfulfilled user is a future negative review, a ranked Reddit thread, a referral that doesn't happen. The infrastructure debt is no longer just a product problem — it's becoming a brand problem.

**The bright spot:** pete's 4-match WC Philadelphia watchlist is the first concrete validation that the WC strategy has a real audience. If we ship price tracking in the next 2 weeks, the WC window (Jun 11 tournament start, 55 days out) is exactly the right size to prove the product works on a high-stakes, time-bounded event slate.

**The worst possible path** is to spend the next 10 days the same way we spent the last 10: infrastructure untouched, features unfixed, daily reports documenting the same unresolved issues. Something has to move on the engineering side next week or the entire marketing apparatus is working on a foundation that doesn't exist.
