## Competitor Deep-Dive: Gametime (Week 4 Rotation)

### Product

**Features Gametime has that we don't:**

- Mobile-first native app (iOS + Android) with "Last-Minute Tickets" as a flagship product
- Real-time "Zone Deals" — dynamic pricing bundles for sections with unsold inventory near event time
- Price drop alerts within the app (push notifications) — our closest direct competitor for this specific feature
- Instant mobile ticket delivery (barcode / Apple Wallet / Google Wallet)
- "Gametime Guarantee" (100% Buyer Guarantee against fakes, cancellations, wrong tickets)
- Interactive seat map with venue-accurate row/section rendering
- Panic-mode UX for <24h-to-event purchases (explicit "buying soon" urgency cues)
- Transparent all-in pricing in-app (fees rolled into headline price; rare in category)
- Strong sports vertical — MLB, NBA, NHL, NFL, MLS, minor leagues all supported
- Stadium & arena guides with parking, food, concourse tips (similar to our venue pages but app-gated)

**Features we have that Gametime doesn't:**

- Multi-source price comparison (Gametime shows only Gametime listings)
- Web-first experience with full functionality (Gametime's web is a shadow of their app)
- Price history and trend analysis (when infrastructure is fixed)
- Buy/wait/hold recommendation engine — unique to us
- SEO-driven discovery (78+ indexed pages) — Gametime is app-store-driven, low organic footprint
- Free account with zero transaction friction (Gametime has purchase-gated features)
- Venue and city guides as standalone content (not locked behind app install)

**UX/UI differences:**

- Gametime: app-native, large buttons, aggressive urgency cues ("Only 2 tickets left at this price!"), gamified purchase moments, map-first navigation
- TicketScan: web-native, comparison-first layout, data-dense, no urgency/gamification — the "serious buyer" aesthetic

**Pricing model:**

- Gametime: marketplace with all-in pricing (no fee surprise at checkout — genuine differentiator vs StubHub/Vivid)
- TicketScan: free comparison, no transaction fees (affiliate attribution when we send to ticketing sites)

### Marketing

**SEO:**

- Gametime's organic SEO is **comparatively weak** — their org discovery strategy is app-store ASO, not web SERP. Ranks for "last-minute [event] tickets" and "[event] tickets tonight" niches, but misses broad "[event] tickets" head terms.
- **Gap for us:** "last-minute Cubs tickets," "last-minute concert tickets tonight," "[event] tickets same day" are winnable for a comparison-led site.
- **Gametime's org footprint is maybe 1/10th of StubHub or Vivid Seats' keyword count.** They've traded organic for mobile-first distribution.
- Their comparatively thin web content opens specific long-tail opportunities: informational queries like "is Gametime legit," "Gametime fees," "Gametime alternative," "Gametime vs StubHub," "Gametime vs SeatGeek" all have moderate monthly search volume and low competition.

**Content:**

- Gametime blog is thin — posts are infrequent, mostly tournament/season previews
- Heavy video content (in-app, not blog): behind-the-scenes, fan-POV, gameday vlogs
- TikTok-native content strategy — strong on short-form fan content, weak on written content
- **Gap for us:** Written content (which is what ranks in SERP) is our underserved opportunity. We have 78+ SEO pages; Gametime has ~dozens.

**Social:**

- TikTok: strongest channel — ~1M+ followers, sports-event highlight reels, fan-submitted content
- Instagram: ~300K+, polished sports + event photography
- Twitter/X: ~200K+, sports partnerships, last-minute deal drops
- YouTube: ~100K+, mostly short-form
- **Our gap:** TikTok specifically. Gametime has built a younger-demographic audience there that translates to app downloads. We have no TikTok presence. This is a channel-specific strategic question for Social + Growth.

**Paid:**

- Heavy app-install spend on iOS + Google Play (the primary acquisition channel)
- Sports-event-specific retargeting (someone searches "Cubs tickets" → sees Gametime display + YouTube pre-roll for next 72h)
- Partnerships with teams for in-stadium QR codes and app CTAs — distribution TicketScan cannot replicate
- Lower Google Ads spend on SERP than StubHub/Vivid (consistent with SEO thinness — they don't compete on search intent, they compete on app presence)

**Email:**

- Price drop alerts (strong — their direct competitor to our target-price feature)
- Event reminders ("Your Cubs vs. Reds game is in 3 days!")
- In-app push notifications dominate over email — their email is sparser and transactional

### Positioning

- **Their value prop:** "Best-last-minute tickets, guaranteed, on your phone." Heavy emphasis on (a) speed, (b) mobile, (c) safety.
- **Target audience:** Mobile-native buyers, 18–34, event-decision-made-within-24-hours-of-event.
- **Differentiation:** All-in pricing (no surprise fees) + strong mobile-first UX + last-minute focus. Their guarantee is table stakes; their all-in-pricing is the actual moat within the StubHub/Vivid/Gametime trio.

### Opportunities (Where TicketScan Can Win)

1. **"[event] tickets vs" long-tail cluster** — "Gametime vs StubHub," "Gametime vs SeatGeek," "TicketScan vs Gametime" — comparison queries with high buyer intent and low Gametime organic presence.
2. **Fee transparency is not a unique Gametime moat.** They market all-in pricing, but across Ticketmaster/SeatGeek/StubHub we can show 2–3 sources and force all-in comparison. Our "fee-honest comparison" pitch beats their "fee-honest single source."
3. **Desktop/web buyers are underserved.** Gametime's web experience is a fallback, not a primary product. Serious comparison shoppers — older demographics, group/corporate buyers, high-ticket-price buyers — prefer a desktop, data-dense UI. This is our natural audience segment.
4. **Price history moat** — Gametime shows current price + "X% chance price drops," but they do not show historical price charts. This is our unique asset (once cron is restored).
5. **Venue + city guides outrank their content in search.** Our 24 venue pages + 37 city pages + 17 WC pages = ~78 indexed pages targeting head terms. Gametime has neither the content library nor the SEO investment. This is a distribution advantage we already own.
6. **MLB vertical** — ajvanprooyen's 6-game Cubs cluster is the first MLB season-ticketing signal in our dataset. Gametime is strong in MLB (especially last-minute) but their all-in-pricing ceiling is comparison-vs-one-source. Our MLB content+comparison angle is unclaimed territory.

### Threats

1. **Mobile app gap is our biggest asymmetric vulnerability.** For the 18–34, last-minute buyer segment, Gametime's app + push-notification UX is meaningfully better. We don't have an app at all.
2. **Partnerships with teams/venues** — Gametime has in-stadium QR-code distribution. Every Cubs game at Wrigley is a Gametime billboard we can't match.
3. **TikTok presence** — if younger demo is the growth market, Gametime is already there and we aren't.
4. **All-in pricing is a real user-facing claim.** Users who are price-sensitive *and* anti-surprise-fees go to Gametime first. Our comparison UX assumes users want to shop across sources; Gametime's assumes users want a guaranteed price now.
5. **Infrastructure debt compounds the threat.** Every week our price history stays empty is a week we cannot differentiate from "just another aggregator." Gametime's core value prop (all-in pricing) is already shipping. Ours (price tracking) is not.

## Strategic Assessment

Last week's assessment: "The product is starting to show cracks too." This week's update:

**The cracks are now identifiable patterns, not anecdotes — and acquisition just delivered the user whose Day 8 will test everything.**

Three patterns are now visible, with enough data behind each to commit to the hypothesis:

1. **Non-activation is bimodal.** Users either activate in under 5 minutes (arrive with specific intent) or never activate at all (arrive curious). There is no middle. The UX currently serves the first group perfectly and the second group not at all.
2. **Bursts are unrepeatable without attribution.** Apr 9–10 (3 signups, 8 adds) and Apr 22–23 (5 signups, 7 adds) are our two biggest 48h windows of April. Both are single-digit-signup events and both are unattributed. Without GA, we cannot reproduce either and cannot know whether it's seasonality, SEO wins, external referrers, or coincidence.
3. **Power users follow a specific template.** Four of them now. Single venue, single artist/team/event, full slate, rapid add, minimal return activity. This is the exact user TicketScan's price-comparison + price-history + alerts stack was designed to serve — and it's the exact user the broken infrastructure is currently failing.

**The priority stack has one change from last week: Apr 30 is now a hard deadline.**

1. **Fix price-tracking cron by Apr 30** — ajvanprooyen's Cubs home-stand opens May 1
2. Fix drip campaign (unblocks ≥24 queued emails across 19 users)
3. Connect GA4 API (unblocks attribution, enables reproduction of bursts)
4. Post-signup UX audit + "pick your first event" flow (unblocks the non-activator segment)
5. Ship 24h post-signup nudge email (cheapest retention hook)
6. Target-price UX fix (unblocks alerts — the feature users subscribed for)
7. Then — and only then — invest in acquisition

**Strategic question to surface this week:** With 5 non-activators in a 10-day window and 17 days of infrastructure silence, **every new signup today has a ~50% chance of being a non-activator and a ~100% chance of hitting a silent product failure if they do activate.** Continued acquisition investment — paid or organic — compounds the cost of every infrastructure day not shipped. The honest framing is that we are currently optimizing for the wrong loop.

**The bright spots:**

- Apr 22–23 was the biggest signup-cluster of April, and 3 of 5 users are still in the activation window (Apr 26–27 will decide). If they convert, April finishes above March.
- ajvanprooyen represents the strongest single-user validation of the MLB/season-ticketing thesis we've ever had.
- Bruno Mars just validated the programmatic-SEO template at the multi-user, multi-venue scale we were hoping for.
- Chicago overtook New York this week as our highest-demand metro — entirely from organic signup behavior, no paid, no campaign.

**The worst-case scenario** is that May 1 arrives, ajvanprooyen returns to the dashboard, sees no price data, no trend chart, no alert, no recommendation — and writes TicketScan off. That's the kind of product-failure moment that generates 1-star Reddit threads and ends the strongest acquisition window of the month with a zero in the retention column. Apr 30 is 7 days from now. The operational tempo from Apr 25 → Apr 30 determines whether the month's best acquisition win turns into its most expensive churn.
