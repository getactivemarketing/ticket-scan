## Competitor Deep-Dive: Gametime (Week 4 rotation)

### Product

**What they do:** Mobile-first, last-minute ticket marketplace. Core UX is built around buying tickets 2 hours before the game/show — not planning weeks out.

| Feature | Gametime | TicketScan |
|---------|----------|------------|
| Price comparison across sources | No (own inventory) | Yes (TM, SG, SH) |
| Price history / trend | No | Yes (tracking, not displaying) |
| Price alerts | No | Yes (broken — no target input) |
| Last-minute focus | Yes (core identity) | No |
| Mobile app | Yes (iOS + Android) | No |
| "All-in pricing" (fees shown upfront) | Yes (key differentiator) | No |
| Seat view photos | Yes | No |

### Marketing

- **SEO:** Ranks strongly for "[city] [team] last minute tickets" and "cheap [event] tickets tonight." TicketScan has no content strategy around last-minute angle.
- **Value prop:** "All-in pricing" + "last-minute deals" — price transparency is their hook, similar to our comparison angle.
- **Paid:** Heavy Google Shopping and App Store ads. We have $0 spend.
- **Social:** Heavy Instagram/TikTok with "deal of the day" posts — verifiable prices, not invented.

### Opportunity for TicketScan

1. **All-in pricing display.** Gametime wins trust by showing final price including fees upfront. Our comparison tables should show all-in, not base price — add fee estimates.
2. **Last-minute angle is uncontested in our content.** A "best last-minute ticket deals this weekend" page (refreshed weekly or daily) would target the Gametime audience before they open the app.
3. **Price history is our moat — but it's dark.** Gametime has zero price history. We have the infrastructure; we just need to fix price tracking so data populates and display it.

### Threats

- Gametime has a real mobile app, real inventory, and real price data. Until our price tracking works, our comparison is a shell. Gametime users get a complete transaction; TicketScan users get a watchlist they can't use.
