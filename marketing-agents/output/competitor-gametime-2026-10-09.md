# Competitor Analysis: Gametime — 2026-10-09

**Rotation:** Gametime (following the prior Vivid Seats review). **Confidence:** high for features explicitly listed by Gametime or Apple; medium for market/SEO conclusions because no rank or ad-spend dataset was available.

## Product

Gametime positions itself as a mobile-first, last-minute resale marketplace. Its official App Store listing states that buyers can purchase up to 90 minutes after an event starts, receive mobile tickets, see venue maps and panoramic seat views, use all-in pricing, and access Gametime Ticket Coverage. The listing also describes a 110% lowest-price guarantee, lightning refunds, job-loss protection, and ticket sharing; restrictions apply.

Its March 2025 product-release post highlights Zone Deals 2.0, Seat Perks, optional Ticket Protection, Ticket Tracker, redesigned checkout, more search paths, faster deal discovery, and AI-powered customer support. These are verified product claims from Gametime’s own materials, not an independent UX test.

### Features Gametime has that TicketScan does not

- Native mobile app and mobile ticket delivery.
- LastCall purchase window extending after event start.
- Seat maps, panoramic seat views, Seat Perks, and 2-click checkout.
- Ticket Coverage / buyer-protection packaging.
- Zone Deals and a 110% lowest-price guarantee.
- Ticket Tracker and AI-powered customer support, per its 2025 release post.

### TicketScan advantages or potential advantages

- Event discovery across the project’s supported sources rather than a single resale marketplace.
- Venue-specific seating, capacity, access, and guide content.
- Onsale and presale date/time information.
- Personal watchlist for revisiting events.
- Editorial and SEO coverage around venues, teams, cities, categories, and World Cup 2026.
- A potentially differentiated neutral research layer once multi-source price ingestion is healthy again. This is a future opportunity, not a current public product claim.

## Marketing and SEO

Gametime’s discoverable structure is broad and programmatic: performer pages, league/category pages, city/event pages, editorial guides, and app-store presence. Its content repeatedly reinforces the same commercial promise—last-minute value, mobile convenience, and buyer confidence—rather than leading with a neutral comparison narrative.

The clearest SEO opportunity for TicketScan is not to imitate Gametime’s resale inventory. It is to own research intent around:

- venue seating and section guides;
- presale and onsale calendars;
- “how to compare ticket fees” and all-in-price education;
- event discovery by city, team, artist, and venue;
- transparent “TicketScan vs. Gametime” comparison intent.

No current keyword ranking, paid-search copy, social engagement, or email-frequency claim was verified in this review.

## Positioning

Gametime: “last-minute value and confidence in a mobile buying flow.”

TicketScan: “find the event, understand the venue and onsale timing, save it for later, and follow a seller link when ready.”

The strategic distinction is marketplace execution versus independent ticket research. TicketScan should not copy Gametime’s “lowest price” or protection promises; it should make the research workflow more useful and more trustworthy.

## Opportunities

1. Publish `/compare/ticketscan-vs-gametime` with a factual feature matrix and a prominent disclosure that TicketScan is not the seller or guarantor of a ticket transaction.
2. Build “last-minute ticket checklist” content that explains delivery timing, fees, venue entry, seller protection, and speculative listings without promising a TicketScan price signal.
3. Make venue guides the bridge from informational search to event discovery: seating zones, capacity, entrances, bag rules, and onsale dates.
4. When feeds recover, evaluate a neutral all-in comparison that includes Gametime only if a supported data/affiliate path is available; do not hard-code a claim before verifying inventory and fees.
5. Add outbound seller-click tracking so the team can measure whether research pages lead to useful actions.

## Threats

- Gametime’s mobile UX, seat-view tooling, protection language, and last-minute brand are difficult to match with a web-only research product.
- Its 2025 release post shows a steady product-iteration cadence, including support, delivery visibility, and checkout improvements.
- “Last-minute value” is a simple message that can outperform a complex comparison pitch unless TicketScan makes its research benefit immediately obvious.
