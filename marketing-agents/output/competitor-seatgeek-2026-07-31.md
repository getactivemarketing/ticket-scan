# Competitor Analysis: SeatGeek — July 31, 2026

_Week 1 of the four-week rotation. Findings checked against SeatGeek’s current public product and editorial pages on July 31, 2026._

## Product

### SeatGeek strengths TicketScan does not currently match

- Transaction-ready marketplace with native inventory, checkout, delivery workflow, buyer protection, and mobile app.
- Interactive seat maps, seat-view context, filters, Instant Delivery indicators, and Deal Score.
- All-in pricing is presented before checkout, with taxes/shipping/optional items called out separately where applicable.
- Track Prices / price alerts are customer-facing and tied to event discovery.

SeatGeek’s own pages describe Deal Score as a 1–10 value signal using price, seat location, expected view, historical prices, and comparable listings; its all-in-pricing page also promotes Track Prices for users who want to wait. Sources: [Deal Score and price tracking](https://seatgeek.com/blog/how-to-track-price-drops-and-find-the-best-ticket-deals-on-seatgeek), [all-in pricing](https://seatgeek.com/blog/seatgeek-all-in-pricing-explained-how-to-avoid-hidden-ticket-fees), [last-minute MLB workflow](https://seatgeek.com/blog/last-minute-mlb-tickets-how-to-buy-safely-before-first-pitch).

### TicketScan strengths

- Cross-source comparison positioning across Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, and StubHub rather than a single marketplace.
- Price-history and buy/wait recommendation infrastructure.
- Venue, city, World Cup, blog, and SEO landing-page coverage.
- Source-neutral outbound buying concept; users can choose the seller after comparison.

### UX/UI difference

SeatGeek optimizes for fast selection and checkout. TicketScan optimizes for research, comparison, and future purchase timing. At present, TicketScan’s research advantage is not visible enough because price history is populated for only one event and target-price alerts cannot be configured.

### Pricing model

SeatGeek monetizes completed marketplace transactions and communicates all-in pricing. TicketScan is a free comparison/referral product; the user should see an honest estimated all-in price and a clear outbound path without implying TicketScan processes the sale.

## Marketing

- **SEO/content:** SeatGeek is publishing current, intent-led guides around last-minute concerts, NFL, MLB, presales, delivery, all-in pricing, and buyer safety. Recent examples include [last-minute concert tickets](https://seatgeek.com/blog/can-you-trust-last-minute-concert-tickets-what-to-know-before-buying), [NFL before kickoff](https://seatgeek.com/blog/buying-last-minute-nfl-tickets-how-to-find-a-good-deal-before-kickoff), and [missed presales](https://seatgeek.com/blog/missed-the-presale-heres-your-guide-to-securing-tickets-after-early-access-ends).
- **Positioning:** speed, trust, value scoring, and transaction completion. “Deal Score” makes an abstract value judgment tangible.
- **Social/paid/email:** public repository inspection cannot verify current engagement, spend, cadence, or creative performance. Do not invent benchmarks; use a manual ad-library/social audit next week.
- **Editorial gap for TicketScan:** a recurring “compare before you buy” series can explain when SeatGeek’s inventory is attractive versus Ticketmaster/StubHub, while highlighting TicketScan’s cross-source history.

## Positioning

SeatGeek targets fans who want to buy quickly with confidence, especially last-minute and mobile-first buyers. TicketScan should target deliberate comparison shoppers: fans who have a budget, multiple seller options, or enough lead time to monitor price movement.

## Opportunities

1. Ship all-in estimated prices beside every source, with a clear “estimate” label and fee methodology.
2. Make a Deal-Score-like “TicketScan Value” signal based on cross-source rank, section tier, price history, and fee-adjusted price; disclose the inputs.
3. Build pages for “SeatGeek vs TicketScan,” “compare tickets with fees,” “last-minute ticket price tracker,” and event + city comparison queries.
4. Add a last-minute mode that ranks delivery readiness and price movement, without pretending TicketScan owns inventory.
5. Fix price tracking and alerts before promoting the data moat.

## Threats

- SeatGeek owns the transaction, inventory, mobile delivery, and buyer-support relationship.
- Its editorial machine is already covering the same high-intent questions TicketScan wants to rank for.
- Deal Score and all-in pricing can make a single-marketplace choice feel simpler than a comparison workflow.

## Recommended response

Do not copy SeatGeek’s marketplace model. Differentiate around transparent cross-source intelligence: “See every source, fees included; know whether today’s price is actually good; set a budget and watch.” Make that promise true through target-price UI, event normalization, populated price history, and GA4 measurement.
