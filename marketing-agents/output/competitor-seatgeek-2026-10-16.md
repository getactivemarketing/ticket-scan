# Competitor Analysis: SeatGeek — 2026-10-16

**Rotation:** SeatGeek. **Research method:** official SeatGeek pages scraped with Firecrawl on 2026-10-16, plus repository comparison against current TicketScan capabilities. **Confidence:** high for features and claims stated by SeatGeek itself; medium for SEO/paid/social conclusions because no rank or ad-spend dataset was available.

## Product

SeatGeek is both a ticket marketplace and a primary-ticketing/enterprise technology company. Its consumer homepage emphasizes event discovery, “Ask SeatGeek,” trending events, sports/concert/theater categories, city pages, and a large programmatic performer/league/venue taxonomy.

The most material current product moves are:

- **Ask SeatGeek:** a conversational search experience for fans, announced in August 2026. This pushes SeatGeek beyond keyword search toward natural-language discovery.
- **TourIQ:** announced October 2, 2026, for promoters and venues. SeatGeek says it uses historical primary/resale transactions, demand signals, and comparable events to value a show and generate seat-level scaling. This is a B2B intelligence product, not a TicketScan consumer feature to copy immediately.
- **Consumer marketplace:** the current homepage exposes event discovery, categories, city pages, trending events, and seller checkout paths. SeatGeek owns the transaction, delivery, and marketplace experience.

### Features SeatGeek has that TicketScan does not

- Native marketplace checkout and ticket inventory.
- Conversational AI search via Ask SeatGeek.
- Seat-level marketplace and primary-ticketing infrastructure.
- Consumer-scale event, performer, city, league, and venue taxonomy.
- Enterprise pricing/intelligence products for promoters and venues.

### TicketScan strengths / defensible differences

- Independent research layer with venue-specific seating, capacity, access, and planning guides.
- Onsale and presale date/time information.
- Saved-event watchlist for returning to events.
- Cross-source event discovery with links out to Ticketmaster and TicketNetwork.
- Editorial coverage designed to answer “what should I check before buying?” rather than complete the transaction.

TicketScan should not claim live price comparison, price history, alerts, or buy-timing recommendations while the product-status outage remains active.

## Marketing and SEO

SeatGeek’s observable information architecture is broad and commercially direct:

- sports, concert, theater, and other category hubs;
- city pages with event-type subpages;
- performer, team, league, venue, and event pages;
- editorial/press pages for product launches and ticket-buying education;
- a consumer homepage that rotates trending events and discovery prompts.

The SEO implication is not to clone SeatGeek’s marketplace taxonomy. TicketScan should own adjacent research intent where verified facts are more useful than inventory breadth: seating sections, capacity, venue entrances, onsale/presale timing, fee checklists, and seller-risk education.

No current keyword ranking, paid-ad copy, email frequency, or social engagement level was verified in this review.

## Positioning

**SeatGeek:** discover and buy tickets in a marketplace, with conversational search and an increasingly explicit data/AI story.

**TicketScan:** research an event, understand the venue and onsale timing, save it for later, and follow a seller link when ready.

The clearest strategic distinction is transaction ownership versus independent decision support. TicketScan should make the research workflow faster and more trustworthy, not imply that it can match SeatGeek’s live inventory or marketplace guarantees.

## Opportunities

1. Publish `/compare/ticketscan-vs-seatgeek` with a factual feature matrix and a clear disclosure that TicketScan is not the ticket seller or transaction guarantor.
2. Add “Ask before you buy” venue content: seating-section terminology, entry/access notes, delivery questions, all-in total checklist, and speculative-listing warnings.
3. Use TicketScan’s city/venue/team inventory to create internal-link hubs around high-interest saved venues, especially Madison Square Garden, Sphere, Wrigley Field, Barclays Center, and AT&T Stadium.
4. Make the event-search CTA concrete: “Find an event” / “Check the venue” / “Save an event,” rather than unsupported price-signal language.
5. Add outbound seller-click tracking so TicketScan can measure whether research pages create useful downstream actions.

## Threats

- SeatGeek’s marketplace owns the final conversion and has much richer transaction data.
- Ask SeatGeek compresses discovery and recommendation into a simpler consumer story.
- TourIQ reinforces SeatGeek’s category association with ticket intelligence; TicketScan must avoid vague “AI/data-powered” claims it cannot substantiate.
- SeatGeek’s programmatic page footprint creates substantial search competition for event, city, performer, and team queries.
