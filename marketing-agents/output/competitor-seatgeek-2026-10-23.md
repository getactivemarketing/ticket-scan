# Competitor Analysis: SeatGeek — 2026-10-23

**Rotation:** SeatGeek. **Method:** Firecrawl scrape/search of SeatGeek’s official homepage, press index, Ask SeatGeek announcement, TourIQ announcement, and representative event page, plus repository comparison against current TicketScan capabilities. Confidence is high for SeatGeek’s own product claims and medium for SEO/paid/social conclusions because no rank, ad-spend, or engagement dataset was available.

## Product

SeatGeek combines a consumer ticket marketplace with primary-ticketing and enterprise intelligence products. The official site exposes sports, concerts, theater, city, performer, venue, and event discovery surfaces, interactive seat maps, checkout, resale, seat views, Deal Score, mobile delivery, and Buyer Guarantee language on event pages.

Recent strategic moves:

- **Ask SeatGeek** brings conversational AI search to the marketplace, according to SeatGeek’s August 26, 2026 announcement.
- **TourIQ** launched October 2, 2026 for promoters and venues. SeatGeek says it uses historical primary/resale transactions, demand signals, and comparable events to value a show and generate seat-level scaling.
- SeatGeek’s press index also lists new ticketing-technology partnerships, including Nashville SC on October 7, 2026.

### Features SeatGeek has that TicketScan does not

- Native marketplace inventory, checkout, delivery, resale, and buyer support.
- Conversational consumer search via Ask SeatGeek.
- Interactive seat maps, seat views, and Deal Score-style value guidance.
- Large programmatic taxonomy of performers, teams, leagues, cities, venues, and events.
- Primary-ticketing and promoter/venue intelligence products.

### TicketScan’s defensible differences

- Independent event research with venue-specific sections, capacity, access, and planning guidance.
- Onsale and presale dates and times.
- Saved-event workflow for returning to an event.
- Event discovery with links out to Ticketmaster and TicketNetwork.
- Editorial guidance on fees, speculative listings, and venue-specific buying context.

TicketScan must not claim live price comparison, price history, alerts, or buy-timing recommendations while the product-status outage remains active.

## Marketing and SEO

SeatGeek’s information architecture is commercially direct and broad: category hubs, cities, teams, performers, venues, event pages, editorial/press pages, and purchase paths. Its event pages combine transactional intent with templated FAQ and venue content, creating a large search footprint.

TicketScan should own adjacent research intent instead of cloning the marketplace taxonomy:

- “What section is best at [venue]?”
- “[Venue] capacity, entrances, and seating guide.”
- “[Event/team] onsale and presale dates.”
- “How to compare all-in ticket fees.”
- “TicketScan vs SeatGeek” with a factual, disclosed feature matrix.

No current SeatGeek keyword rankings, paid-ad copy, email frequency, or social engagement level was verified in this review.

## Positioning

**SeatGeek:** discover and buy tickets in a marketplace, with conversational search and an increasingly explicit data/AI story.

**TicketScan:** research an event, understand the venue and onsale timing, save it for later, and follow a seller link when ready.

The strategic distinction is transaction ownership versus independent decision support. TicketScan should make research faster and more trustworthy; it should not imply marketplace guarantees or live pricing that it cannot currently provide.

## Opportunities

1. Publish the comparison page at `/compare/ticketscan-vs-seatgeek` with a clear research-vs-marketplace decision path.
2. Build “before you buy” venue modules: section terminology, access/entry notes, delivery questions, all-in total checklist, and speculative-listing warnings.
3. Use current high-interest saved venues — Madison Square Garden, Sphere, Wrigley Field, Barclays Center, and AT&T Stadium — as internal-link hubs.
4. Make CTAs concrete: “Find an event,” “Check the venue,” and “Save an event.”
5. Add outbound seller-click tracking so the research layer can prove downstream value.

## Threats

- SeatGeek owns the final transaction and has richer first-party marketplace data.
- Ask SeatGeek compresses discovery and recommendation into a simpler consumer story.
- TourIQ reinforces SeatGeek’s association with ticket intelligence; TicketScan should avoid vague “AI/data-powered” claims.
- SeatGeek’s programmatic page footprint creates substantial competition for event, city, performer, team, and venue queries.
