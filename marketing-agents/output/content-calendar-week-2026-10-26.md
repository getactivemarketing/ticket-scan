## Production notes

### P0 — All-in checkout checklist

Lead with a compact checklist: confirm event/date/venue, compare the same section and seat notes, identify primary versus resale listing, check delivery method, expand fees and taxes, review refund/cancellation terms, and save the confirmation. CTA: “Get the printable ticket-buying checklist.” Do not say TicketScan displays all-in prices unless the live implementation is verified.

### P0 — Venue map refresh

Every refresh should use the venue’s existing verified `venue.sections`, capacity, entry/arrival notes, and FAQs. Do not add generic “best seats” claims where the data does not support them. Confirm current event dates through the live event feed before publication.

### P1 — Seasonal sports pages

Keep these pages event-discovery-first: who is playing, where, when, onsale/presale details, venue sections, and a buyer checklist. Avoid “cheap,” “best time to buy,” “price trend,” “savings,” or comparative dollar claims unless independently sourced and clearly not attributed to TicketScan.

## Competitor gap hypotheses to test

These are research hypotheses, not measured conclusions. SeatGeek and StubHub commonly compete on marketplace education and event-specific buying explainers. TicketScan’s defensible gap is a structured combination of verified venue facts, local event discovery, onsale timing, and plain-language checkout/safety guidance.

1. Build a venue-first path from search landing → seating sections → current events → official seller link.
2. Add calendar/time-zone clarity to presale and onsale explainers.
3. Make listing type, delivery, fees, and refund terms explicit in every checklist.
4. Use World Cup venue pages for practical arrival and section guidance rather than speculative ticket-price content.
