## The Postseason Is Here — and So Is the Markup

The 2026 NBA and NHL playoffs kicked off this week, and if you've already checked ticket prices, you know the drill: face value sold out in minutes, resale listings appeared at 2-3x the price, and every marketplace is showing a different number for the same seat.

First-round NBA playoff tickets are starting around $85-$150 on secondary markets for upper-bowl seats. Lower bowl? You're looking at $250-$600 depending on the matchup. NHL first-round seats follow a similar pattern, with some markets (looking at you, Original Six cities) commanding a premium that makes World Cup group-stage tickets look reasonable.

Here's the thing: those prices are not fixed. They move every day — sometimes every hour — and the cheapest source flips constantly between Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, and StubHub. If you buy the first listing you see, you're almost certainly overpaying.

## Why Playoff Tickets Are the Ultimate Comparison Shopping Game

Regular-season tickets have relatively stable pricing. You know roughly what a Tuesday Nets game costs versus a Saturday Lakers game. Playoffs throw all of that out the window because of three factors:

### 1. Series uncertainty drives price swings

A Game 1 ticket for a first-round series between the 1-seed and 8-seed prices very differently from a Game 5 in a 2-2 series. If the favored team wins Games 1 and 2 at home, Games 3 and 4 tickets on the road often drop — sellers get nervous about a sweep. But if the underdog steals a road game? Prices spike 30-50% overnight for the remaining home games.

This means the window to buy is narrow, and the price you see on Monday might not exist by Wednesday.

### 2. Marketplace arbitrage is real

Each marketplace has different seller pools. Ticketmaster's Verified Resale tends to have higher ask prices because sellers know it's the "official" platform. SeatGeek often has competitive pricing from professional brokers. StubHub's volume means you'll occasionally find a panicking seller who listed below market.

We pulled pricing on a first-round NBA matchup across all three platforms and found a $127 spread for comparable seats in the same section. Same game. Same row. Different price depending on where you click "buy."

### 3. Fees change the math

The listed price is never the real price. Ticketmaster's fees average 20-25% on top. StubHub runs 25-30%. SeatGeek's "all-in pricing" toggle helps, but not every listing uses it. A ticket that looks $40 cheaper on one platform might actually be $15 more expensive after fees.

This is exactly why side-by-side comparison matters more for playoffs than any other time of year.

## The Smart Buyer's Playoff Ticket Playbook

### Check all three platforms before you buy anything

This sounds obvious. Almost nobody does it. Pull up TicketScan's comparison tool, enter the game, and look at what Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, and StubHub are actually charging — fees included. The savings from a 90-second comparison average $40-$80 per ticket on playoff games.

### Set a price alert for the games you want

Don't sit there refreshing three tabs. Add the game to your watchlist with a target price — the maximum you'd pay — and let the system notify you when any marketplace hits it. Playoff prices are volatile. Dips happen. You just have to be ready when they do.

### Know when prices typically drop

Playoff ticket pricing follows a predictable curve for most first-round games:

- **Highest:** The day matchups are announced (right now)
- **First dip:** 2-3 days before the game, when season-ticket holders who can't attend start listing
- **Second dip:** 4-6 hours before tip-off, when sellers get desperate to recoup something rather than eat the ticket
- **Exception:** Elimination games — these only go up

If you're flexible on which game in the series you attend, wait for the series to develop. Games 3-4 of a lopsided series are often the cheapest playoff tickets you'll find all postseason.

### Watch the matchup, not just the team

A Celtics first-round game against the 8-seed will price differently than if they'd drawn the 7-seed with a star player. Casual fans buy based on team name. Smart buyers look at the matchup narrative. A "rivalry rematch" or "upset alert" series will command premium pricing even in the first round.

## NHL Playoffs: Same Rules, Different Wrinkles

Everything above applies to hockey, with two additions:

**Home ice matters more for pricing.** NHL playoff atmospheres are legendary, and the home team's fans will pay a premium that NBA crowds don't always match. That means away games can be significantly cheaper if you're willing to travel.

**Fewer games means higher stakes per ticket.** Best-of-seven still, but NHL first-round games feel bigger because the sport gets less regular-season attention. Expect fewer price dips and faster sellouts for elimination games.

The San Jose Sharks announced playoff tickets go on sale April 10 — one of the few teams where you can still get face-value seats in the first round. If you're in the Bay Area, that's a rare opportunity.

## The Bottom Line

Playoff tickets are a seller's market, but sellers disagree on the price. That disagreement is your advantage — if you bother to look at more than one marketplace.

The fans who get fleeced are the ones who see a listing, feel the urgency, and click "buy" without checking anywhere else. The fans who save $100+ per ticket are the ones who compare prices across platforms, set alerts for dips, and buy during the predictable windows when sellers get nervous.

The games are starting now. The prices are moving now. Don't buy the first number you see.
