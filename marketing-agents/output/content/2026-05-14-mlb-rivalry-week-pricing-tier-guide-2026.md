If you read [yesterday's piece on Yankees vs Red Sox at Yankee Stadium](https://www.ticketscan.io/blog/yankees-vs-red-sox-yankee-stadium-rivalry-pricing-may-2026), you already know the headline: a Section 203 Bleacher seat that runs $32 against the Tigers cleared $128 at first pitch against Boston. That is a 4x premium on the cheapest seat in the building. The average across the stadium was closer to 2.2x.

What yesterday's piece did not cover is that **Yankees–Red Sox is the most expensive rivalry premium in MLB** — and the second-place rivalry runs noticeably less. The third-place rivalry runs less than that. By the time you get to the sixth tier of MLB rivalries, the "rivalry premium" is small enough that you might not notice it without checking historical data.

We pulled three full seasons of resale floor data across Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, and StubHub for the twelve most-cited MLB rivalry series. The premium versus a baseline same-month weeknight against a non-rivalry visitor sorts into six clean tiers. If you are budgeting for a rivalry-week trip, this is the table to start with.

Three things to understand before you read the worked examples below.

**First, the premium is on the resale floor, not the face value.** Face value moves much less between rivalry and non-rivalry games (it is set in November, before the matchup matters). The 2x to 2.5x premium on Yankees–Red Sox is happening on StubHub, SeatGeek, and Vivid Seats listings, not in MLB's primary box office.

**Second, the premium is not evenly distributed across sections.** The visiting-fan-density premium concentrates in different section bands for different rivalries. Bostonians at Yankee Stadium cluster in the Bleachers and the 400-Level uppers. Cardinals fans at Wrigley cluster in the Bleachers and the upper bowl behind home plate. Giants fans at Dodger Stadium cluster in the Field Level along the third-base line because NorCal transplants in LA disproportionately buy "the seat they actually want," not "the cheapest entry." The section-band pattern is rivalry-specific, and it changes your buy-strategy by stadium.

**Third, platform variance is not uniform either.** Some rivalries reliably skew to StubHub being the cheapest source; others skew to SeatGeek or Vivid Seats. The skew is driven by which platform the visiting fan base disproportionately uses, and it is more predictable than it sounds.

## Tier 1: Yankees vs Red Sox (2.0x to 2.5x)

The headline rivalry. Yesterday's deep dive covers the section breakdown. The two-line summary: Bleachers and 400-Level uppers carry the steepest premium (2.4x to 2.7x), Main Level corners next (2.3x to 2.5x), Field Level mild (1.4x), Legends Suite flat. The visiting-fan cluster is the Bleachers and the upper-deck 200s.

Platform pattern: **on rivalry games, StubHub's cheap inventory dries up first**, inverting the usual Yankees-tickets-are-cheapest-on-StubHub baseline. SeatGeek's Deal Score over-favors Bleachers because the algorithm does not penalize visiting-fan-density. Vivid Seats is the swing variable.

**Budget worked example:** A Saturday-night Yankees vs Red Sox at Yankee Stadium in August. Bleacher 203: budget $90 to $150 (vs $35 to $80 baseline). 400-Level corner: $80 to $180 (vs $35 to $80 baseline). Main Level 220s: $250 to $480 (vs $110 to $220 baseline). Field Level Legends: $400 to $800 (vs $300 to $600 baseline — the rivalry barely moves this tier).

## Tier 2: Dodgers vs Giants (1.7x to 2.2x)

The West Coast rivalry. Smaller spread than Yankees–Red Sox, but Dodger Stadium's larger capacity means more total premium-priced inventory clears the resale market on Giants visits than any other rivalry in baseball.

The section concentration pattern is different. Giants fans in LA tend to be NorCal transplants with disposable income, and they buy Field Level on the third-base line (the visiting dugout side) — not the cheap seats. The Field Level premium runs 1.8x to 2.0x, the steepest of any non-Yankees–Red Sox rivalry on a premium-tier section. Reserved Level (the 100s) and Loge Level (200s) cluster 1.7x to 2.0x. The Top Deck (uppermost cheap seats) is the *softest* premium on this rivalry, around 1.4x to 1.6x.

Platform pattern: **SeatGeek typically cheapest** on Dodgers vs Giants because SeatGeek's NorCal user-base density (driven by Bay Area early-adopter penetration) means more Giants fans dump tickets on SeatGeek when plans change. StubHub second; Vivid Seats third.

**Budget worked example:** Dodgers vs Giants, 6/13 to 6/15 at Dodger Stadium (T-30 days from today). Top Deck: $35 to $75 (vs $22 to $50 baseline). Reserved Level: $90 to $180 (vs $50 to $110 baseline). Loge: $160 to $310 (vs $90 to $180 baseline). Field Level third-base side: $400 to $850 (vs $220 to $450 baseline — the visiting-fan-density premium concentrating section).

## Tier 3: Cubs vs Cardinals (1.5x to 2.0x)

The classic NL Central rivalry. Cardinals fans travel deeper into Wrigley than almost any visiting fan base in baseball — Section 503 to 535 (the upper bowl behind home plate) and the Bleacher Cap (sections 305 and 311) routinely run 50% Cardinals on visits.

Section pattern: Bleachers 1.7x to 2.0x (the Bleacher Cap is the steepest, the field-level Bleachers softer because Cubs season-ticket holders dominate). Upper bowl 1.6x to 1.9x. Field-level box seats only 1.3x to 1.5x. Cardinals fans buy where they can afford to sit; the cheap seats dominate the premium.

Platform pattern: **Vivid Seats often cheapest** for Cubs vs Cardinals games because Vivid Seats has historically had stronger St. Louis-market penetration than StubHub. SeatGeek second; StubHub third (and StubHub's Cubs inventory dries up first on rivalry games, similar to the Yankees pattern).

**Budget worked example:** Cubs vs Cardinals 6/06 to 6/08 at Wrigley (T-23 days). Bleacher 304: $90 to $150 (vs $50 to $90 baseline). Upper Reserve 528: $55 to $110 (vs $30 to $65 baseline). Field Box: $180 to $360 (vs $130 to $260 baseline). See [our Wrigley Field venue guide](https://www.ticketscan.io/venues/wrigley-field) for the section-by-section diagram.

## Tier 4: Mets vs Phillies and the Lone Star Series (1.4x to 1.8x)

Two rivalries cluster here. Mets vs Phillies has the heat of an NL East division race in most seasons; Astros vs Rangers has the geographic intensity of a 240-mile interstate trip and the recent World Series stakes from the 2023 ALCS.

Section pattern (both rivalries): the premium is more uniform across section bands than Tiers 1 through 3. Visiting-fan-density is high but spread out — Phillies fans take both Field Level and Promenade at Citi Field; Rangers fans take Crawford Boxes and Upper Deck at Minute Maid. The uniform spread means the rivalry premium is *less concentrated* and easier to plan around — no single section is a 2x trap.

Platform pattern: **StubHub typically cheapest** for both rivalries because both visiting fan bases (Philadelphia, Dallas/Fort Worth) skew StubHub-heavy by metro penetration. SeatGeek second; Vivid Seats third.

**Budget worked example:** Mets vs Phillies 6/27 to 6/29 at Citi Field (T-44 days). Promenade Reserved: $40 to $75 (vs $25 to $50 baseline). Excelsior Level: $80 to $150 (vs $50 to $100 baseline). Field Level: $180 to $340 (vs $120 to $230 baseline).

## Tier 5: Mariners vs Astros (1.2x to 1.5x)

The geographic AL West rivalry with the most consistent in-stadium energy. Smaller premium than the Tiers above because the visiting-fan travel pool is smaller — Houston-to-Seattle is a four-hour flight, and most Astros fans who travel to Seattle are the dedicated traveling-fan tier, not the casual rivalry-game weekend buyer.

Section pattern: mild concentration in the View Level (300s) and the Bleachers; Main Level and Diamond Club only marginally elevated. Platform pattern: StubHub and SeatGeek roughly tied; Vivid Seats softer.

**Budget worked example:** Mariners vs Astros 5/22 to 5/24 at T-Mobile Park (T-8 days — the first major rivalry on the week-of-5/18 calendar). View Level 327: $30 to $55 (vs $22 to $42 baseline). Main Level 137: $70 to $130 (vs $50 to $95 baseline). Diamond Club: $250 to $420 (vs $200 to $340 baseline). This is the rivalry tier where waiting for the day-of-game dip is most reliable — the 1.2x to 1.5x premium compresses faster as first pitch approaches than the higher tiers do.

## Tier 6: The 1.1x to 1.3x cluster

Six rivalries cluster here: White Sox vs Cubs (Crosstown Classic, the most-cited rivalry in this tier but the smallest actual resale premium — Crosstown is more of a community event than a price-pressured game), Reds vs Pirates (Ohio River Rivalry), Brewers vs Cardinals, Tigers vs Twins, Padres vs Dodgers (when framed as an in-state rivalry rather than as part of the Dodgers tier-2 frame), and Phillies vs Braves.

The honest takeaway: **at Tier 6, the rivalry is mostly a marketing label.** The pricing premium is small enough that the day-of-week, weather, and matchup-quality variables (Cy Young starter pitching, contender vs cellar-dweller) move prices more than the rivalry label does. If you are buying a Tier 6 rivalry game, treat it like a normal series and let weather and day-of-week drive your buy timing.
