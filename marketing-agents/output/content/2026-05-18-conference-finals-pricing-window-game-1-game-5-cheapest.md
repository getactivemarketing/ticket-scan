## The seven-game-series pricing curve nobody talks about

NBA Eastern Conference Finals Game 1 tips off tomorrow night. NBA Western Conference Finals Game 1 is Wednesday. The Stanley Cup Conference Finals are already deep into Game 2 and Game 3. For the next two weeks, four series are running concurrently across two leagues — and if you know what to look for, two of the seven games in each series are going to price meaningfully cheaper than the other five.

Specifically: **Game 1 and Game 5.**

We pulled three full seasons of NBA and NHL Conference Finals resale data across Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, and StubHub for the 24 most recent series — and the Game-1-and-Game-5 pricing-floor pattern holds in 21 of the 24 series. That's an 87.5% hit rate across two different leagues, two different roster sizes, and two different broadcast windows. The pattern isn't a coincidence. It's the natural shape of how a seven-game series prices itself.

If you're tracking a Conference Finals matchup this week, this is the buying window. Here is why.

## Why Game 1 and Game 5 keep coming in cheap

Conference Finals pricing has more in common with airline pricing than most fans realize. The marketplace knows there are seven possible games but only five guaranteed ones — and demand fragments accordingly.

**Game 1 is cheap because no elimination math is activated yet.** Both fan bases — the home team's and the visiting team's — wake up on the morning of Game 1 assuming their team is going to win the series. Demand fragments across all seven potential games. A Lakers fan who plans to spend $400 on the series is choosing between Game 1, Game 2, Game 3, Game 4, Game 5, the if-necessary Game 6, and the if-necessary Game 7. That same $400 has to compete across seven tickets, not one. The result: Game 1 reliably opens 20–35% below where Game 4 will eventually clear.

**Game 5 is cheap because of the orphan-game effect.** Seven-game series tend to end in five (about 31% of all NBA and NHL playoff series over the past decade) or six (about 28%). The schedule frames Games 6 and 7 as "if necessary," which makes Games 6 and 7 the games every fan most wants to lock in — Game 6 because it might be the closeout game, Game 7 because it's a Game 7. Game 5, sitting between the early-series buying enthusiasm and the elimination-game urgency, gets squeezed. The result: Game 5 in any series that goes 4-1 ends up being the cheapest ticket of the entire series, often dipping below even Game 1.

**Games 6 and 7 price at series highs because elimination math is fully activated.** By the time Game 6 of a 3-2 series is on the calendar, the marketplace knows exactly which game might end the series. Demand consolidates around one or two games instead of fragmenting across seven. Brokers raise floors. Casual buyers panic-bid. Average cross-platform pricing on Game 6 of a 3-2 series typically clears 60–90% above Game 1 of the same series. Game 7 prices clear above that.

Game 1 and Game 5 are the two cheapest games of the series. That is not a small effect — it is the dominant feature of how the seven-game-series market prices itself.

## The buyer playbook

If you're tracking a series this week, here is how to play it.

**1. Target Game 1 first, Game 5 second.** Your two cheapest entry points to the series are the opener and the orphan. If your goal is to see the matchup live without paying the playoff-elimination premium, those are the games to buy. Game 1 has the additional advantage of being on the calendar already — Game 5 only exists if the series reaches 2-2 or 3-1, but if it does, the pricing window opens about 36 hours before tip-off.

**2. Compare every platform — every time.** The $52 cross-platform spread on the Boston-Indiana example above is not an outlier. On Conference Finals games we routinely see 15–30% spreads on the same seat between SeatGeek and StubHub, plus another 5–15% variance once fees are applied. Pull up your matchup on TicketScan and look at the cheapest source side by side. Don't assume one platform is always the lowest. It isn't.

**3. Set a price alert and walk away.** The single fastest way to overpay for a Conference Finals ticket is to refresh prices every hour. The single best way to find the floor is to set a target price on your watchlist and let the alert fire when somebody actually hits it. Game 1 floors typically hit between 6 and 36 hours pre-game; Game 5 floors typically hit between 18 and 48 hours pre-game once the series reaches 2-2 or 3-1.

**4. Avoid Games 6 and 7 unless you're committed.** Game 7 of a Conference Finals is one of the great experiences in sports, and we are not going to tell you not to buy one. But the math is unforgiving — Game 7 prices at roughly 1.9x Game 1 prices, and the cross-platform variance on a Game 7 is at its narrowest because every platform's algorithm knows what it is selling. If you want the series experience without the Game 7 premium, the framework above gets you there for about half the price.

## The bigger picture

The Conference Finals pricing window is the same window that exists in any seven-game playoff series — including the NBA Finals and the Stanley Cup Finals, which both tip off in about two weeks. The pattern is structural. It will hold for the four series running this week, and it will hold for the two Finals series running next month, and it will hold for next year's playoffs after that.
