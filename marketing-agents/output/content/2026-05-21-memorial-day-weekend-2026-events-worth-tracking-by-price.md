Memorial Day Weekend 2026 falls Saturday May 23 through Monday May 25, and the sports plus concert calendar that lands inside those 72 hours is the densest we have seen all year. Both NBA Conference Finals run Game 3 over the weekend. Both NHL Conference Finals do the same. Three of the biggest MLB rivalries play three-game sets concurrently. And the first major stadium tour of the summer kicks off the way every Memorial Day Weekend kicks off summer touring.

If you are planning to buy tickets to any of it, the next 48 hours are when prices move most. Some events are priced near the floor and will firm before first pitch or tipoff. Some are still priced above where they will settle and will give back another 10 to 20% before the weekend. Knowing which is which is the entire job.

We pulled cross-platform data on the eight events that matter most this weekend. Here is the list, ranked roughly by current price volatility, with a one-line buyer playbook for each.

## How to read the list

Each event below has three things you actually need: what the resale floor looks like right now across Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, and StubHub, where the cheapest seat tends to live, and whether the right move today is to buy, wait, or set a target alert and walk away.

If you are tracking more than one of these, do not buy any of them off the first platform you check. The cross-platform spread on big-event weekends is reliably 15 to 30% on the same seat. Yesterday's piece on the [Conference Finals Game 2 cross-platform pricing window](https://www.ticketscan.io/blog/conference-finals-game-2-cross-platform-pricing-may-2026) walks through exactly how the three marketplaces move differently after a Game 1 result. The same pattern applies here.

## The eight events

### 1. NBA Eastern Conference Finals Game 3

Game 3 is the weekend's biggest single-event resale market. The standard pricing-curve pattern says Game 3 prices firm overnight if the home team won Game 2, and soften noticeably if the road team stole one. As of this morning's data pull, the cheapest upper-bowl corner sits in the $135 to $175 range on SeatGeek, $155 to $200 on StubHub, and $180 to $220 on Ticketmaster Verified Resale.

**Playbook:** SeatGeek tends to be cheapest the morning of Game 3, but cheap inventory dries up fast. Set a watchlist alert at a target $25 under the current SeatGeek floor and let it fire. If it has not fired by 4 p.m. local on game day, the cheap inventory is gone and you are paying the day-of-game premium.

### 2. NBA Western Conference Finals Game 3

The Western series is one game behind the Eastern. Game 2 is Friday, which means Game 3 prices reset over the weekend on the road. The Western Conference's smaller-market arenas usually mean a softer resale floor than the Eastern equivalent — corner uppers in the $110 to $150 range are common for a non-marquee Western series.

**Playbook:** If the road team takes Game 2 Friday, expect a SeatGeek floor drop of 8 to 15% by Saturday morning, the same pattern we tracked in 21 of the last 24 Conference Finals series. That dip lasts about 18 hours before brokers reprice toward the median.

### 3. NHL Eastern Conference Final Game 3 or 4

The hockey Conference Final is deeper into its series than the NBA one, which means the weekend game is likely Game 3 or Game 4 depending on the matchup. Hockey resale floors are softer than NBA equivalents by 30 to 40% at the same seat tier, which makes this the best cheap-seat value of any major-sport playoff game on the weekend calendar.

**Playbook:** Hockey arenas have a real upper-bowl ceiling pricing problem on resale. The 300-level corners often clear under $90 across all three platforms the morning of a Game 3. If you want a playoff atmosphere on a budget this weekend, this is the slot.

### 4. NHL Western Conference Final Game 3 or 4

Same dynamic as the East, with one wrinkle: the Western Conference Final typically draws a smaller broker-driven resale market than the Eastern. That means less platform variance and a tighter cross-platform spread. You will not see a 30% gap between SeatGeek and Ticketmaster on a Western hockey playoff game the way you do on the NBA equivalent.

**Playbook:** Tight spreads mean lower upside from shopping across platforms. Buy from whichever platform you trust most for fee transparency. For the all-in price comparison framework, our [Ticketmaster vs SeatGeek fees breakdown](https://www.ticketscan.io/compare) is the cleanest read.

### 5. Yankees vs Dodgers at Yankee Stadium

The interleague matchup of the year. Yankees vs Dodgers in the Bronx is a Tier-1 pricing event on its own, and it lands on Memorial Day Weekend by the schedule's design. The Bleacher 203 floor against the Dodgers is running $110 to $165, up roughly 3x from the $32 to $50 baseline against a non-rivalry visitor. The Field Level corners are running $480 to $720, up roughly 2.5x.

**Playbook:** This is the rivalry-week pricing pattern from [last week's MLB Rivalry Tier Guide](https://www.ticketscan.io/blog/mlb-rivalry-week-pricing-tier-guide-2026) at maximum intensity. StubHub's cheap Bleacher inventory dries up first; SeatGeek's Deal Score over-favors the Bleachers in rivalry games and is the place to look for the cheapest entry. Vivid Seats is the swing variable on the Field Level.

### 6. Dodgers vs Giants at Dodger Stadium

The West Coast rivalry runs concurrently. Dodger Stadium's capacity means more total inventory clears the resale market on a Giants visit than any other rivalry game in baseball, which keeps the cheap-seat floor accessible even on Memorial Day Weekend. Top Deck seats are running $45 to $85 across platforms; Loge Level $170 to $300.

**Playbook:** SeatGeek is reliably cheapest for this rivalry because of the NorCal user-base density. If you are buying the upper-deck cheap seat, lead with SeatGeek; if you are buying the Field Level on the third-base line where the Giants fans cluster, the better cross-platform check is Vivid Seats and StubHub.

### 7. Cubs vs Cardinals at Wrigley Field

The NL Central rivalry. Cardinals fans travel deeper into Wrigley than almost any visiting fan base in baseball, and Memorial Day Weekend amplifies that. The Bleacher Cap (sections 305 and 311) is running $95 to $160; the Upper Reserve 528 is $60 to $115. Cubs season-ticket holders dominate the field-level boxes, which keeps the visiting-fan premium concentrated in the cheap seats.

**Playbook:** Vivid Seats often shows the cheapest Bleacher entry for Cubs-Cardinals because of stronger St. Louis-market broker penetration. If you are going for the atmosphere, the Bleacher Cap is the seat to buy. For the venue layout and the 48-hour pricing-curve pattern at Wrigley, the [Cubs vs Reds at Wrigley deep dive](https://www.ticketscan.io/blog/cubs-vs-reds-wrigley-field-pricing-48-hours-may-2026) covers the section-by-section read.

### 8. The first major stadium tour kickoff of the summer

Memorial Day Weekend is reliably the first big stadium-tour weekend of the year. This year the headline opener is the Coldplay 2026 stadium run, opening at MetLife on Saturday night. Three-night residencies are now the default for stadium tours (we covered why in [the three-night residency piece earlier this month](https://www.ticketscan.io/blog/stadium-tour-three-night-residencies-2026)), and the opening night of any residency carries a 20 to 35% premium over nights two and three.

**Playbook:** If Saturday is sold out or pricing above your budget, check nights two and three for the same tour. The price gap between opening night and the rest of a residency is the cleanest predictable mispricing in stadium touring. SeatGeek has the most aggressive opening-night broker repricing as the show approaches; StubHub holds firmer.
