## The complete US World Cup price map, ranked by cheapest seat

There are 11 US stadiums hosting World Cup 2026 matches, and FIFA prices the cheapest seat in each one very differently. The floor at Levi's Stadium is $80. The floor at MetLife and SoFi is $150. That is nearly a 2x spread on the *get-in* price alone, before anyone mentions the $10,990 Final ticket.

Over the past several weeks we built a full Category-by-Category price guide for every one of those 11 venues. This is the capstone: all 11 ranked by their FIFA Category 4 Group Stage floor, the cheapest honest way into each building. We also did the thing the official sites and most ticket blogs skip, which is tell you what that cheap seat actually costs once you account for getting there and staying comfortable.

Match counts and rounds are pulled straight from FIFA's published US schedule. Floor prices are the low end of each venue's Category 4 (upper corner and endline) Group Stage range. Knockout matches at a venue carry their own, much higher pricing — the floor column is strictly the cheapest Group Stage get-in.

The pattern is not subtle. The four cheapest floors belong to venues hosting zero or one knockout match. The two most expensive floors belong to MetLife and SoFi, which between them host the Final, a Semifinal, and two Quarterfinals. When a building hosts marquee knockout rounds, FIFA's variable-pricing engine lifts the *entire* price ladder, including the cheap seats at the bottom. Prestige is contagious, and it is expensive.

## The get-in spread, and the honesty layer

Strip the ranking down to its extremes and the gap is clean:

| | Venue | Cat 4 GS floor |
|---|---|---|
| **Three cheapest floors** | Levi's / Gillette / Lumen | $80 / $110 / $110 |
| **Three priciest floors** | NRG / MetLife / SoFi | $135 / $150 / $150 |

That is roughly a 45% premium to get into the marquee-venue cluster versus the bargain cluster, for a seat in the same Category 4 tier watching the same sport. If your goal is to be at a World Cup match in the United States and you are flexible on which one, the cheapest-floor venues save you real money on the ticket itself.

But the ticket is not the whole bill, and this is where the official venue pages go quiet.

**The cheapest floor in the country has the worst transit in the country.** Levi's Stadium lists an $80 seat, the lowest in the tournament. It also has the weakest public-transit access of any US venue: a VTA light-rail transfer that most fans skip in favor of driving and paid parking. Arrowhead, tied for the third-cheapest floor at $110, is the same story — no rail at all, eight miles out on I-70, built for tailgating and car traffic. Add two rideshares at $35–$50 each and that $80 Levi's seat is closer to $160 all-in for a solo traveler.

**The best transit pairs with a mid-priced floor.** Lincoln Financial Field sits seventh on price at $125, but its SEPTA Broad Street Line runs straight from Center City to the stadium for $2.50 each way, about a 15-minute ride. Lumen Field in downtown Seattle and NRG in Houston (METRORail, $1.25) round out the genuinely transit-friendly venues. For a fan without a car, Lincoln Financial's $125 seat may be the cheapest *all-in* World Cup ticket in America once you stop paying for parking and rideshares.

**Roof matters more than the price tag suggests.** Three US venues have retractable roofs — AT&T, Mercedes-Benz, and NRG — and Mercedes-Benz pairs that with the cheapest concessions in the tournament ($2 hot dogs, $5 beer). SoFi and Hard Rock carry fixed canopies. The other six are fully open-air. That is not a luxury question. Several US host cities run 90°F-plus with afternoon thunderstorms in June and July, and a covered seat in Houston or Atlanta is a comfort upgrade you cannot buy at an open-air bowl at any price. If you are heat-sensitive, the roof column may outrank the price column.

## How to actually use this map

Three moves, in order.

**1. Decide what you're optimizing for.** Cheapest ticket, full stop, points you at Levi's or one of the $110 venues — just budget for the car. Cheapest all-in trip points you at Lincoln Financial, Lumen, or NRG, where transit is close to free. Best comfort in a hot host city points you at the retractable-roof venues. There is no single "best value" stadium; there is the one that matches your constraint. The map above tells you which.

**2. Compare every source before you buy, at whichever venue you choose.** FIFA's official platform is not automatically the cheapest, and neither is any single resale marketplace. The cheapest source for a given seat flips constantly, sometimes within the same day, as resale sellers reprice near kickoff. Pull your match up and check Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, StubHub, and FIFA resale side by side before you commit to any of them.

**3. Set a price alert and let the floor come to you.** The Category 4 floor seats — the ones in this ranking — have barely moved in eight weeks, which means at this point they are a buy-or-lose, not a wait-and-save. The expensive Category 1 and 2 inventory is where late discounts actually appear as sellers panic. So if you want a cheap seat, set your target at the current floor and buy when it hits. If you want a premium seat, set an alert below face value and wait out the sellers. Either way, you stop refreshing the page and let the alert do the watching.

The reason this map exists is simple: the cheapest way into the 2026 World Cup is knowable, venue by venue, and almost nobody is telling you the all-in version of it. Now you have it.
