## The honest answer to "should I buy now?" is "it depends which seat you want"

We are nine days from the June 11 opener. If you have been waiting for prices to fall before you commit, this is the week the waiting game changes shape.

Here is the thing most last-minute guides get wrong: they give you one answer. Buy now, or hold out. But a World Cup ticket isn't one product. A Category 4 group-stage seat and a Category 1 seat to the same match behave like two completely different markets in the final stretch, and lumping them together is how people either overpay or get shut out.

So split your decision by category before you do anything else.

**The cheap seats are a buy-or-lose.** The Category 4 floor across the US venues runs from about $80 at Levi's up to roughly $150 at MetLife and SoFi. Those numbers have barely moved in weeks, and they are not going to soften now. There is no scenario where the cheapest get-in to a sold-out tournament drops the week before kickoff. Demand is something like 80 times supply. If you want a Cat-4 seat and you can afford it, the move is to buy it, not to wait for a discount that the math says isn't coming.

**The expensive seats are the opposite.** Category 1 and 2 inventory is where the air is. Plenty of those tickets were bought months ago by people who assumed they'd attend, or by resellers who assumed they'd flip at a markup. As kickoff closes in and the flip doesn't happen, some of those sellers start cutting. That's where resale platforms quietly undercut FIFA's official price. If you're after premium seats, this is the week to hold, watch, and let the panic work for you.

One market is a closing door. The other is a slow leak. Treat them differently.

## Your week-by-week plan

The whole game right now is information and patience. You want to know the real cross-platform price for your match before you spend, and you want a system that watches it so you don't have to refresh tabs at midnight.

That means comparing all three resale sources (Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, StubHub) against FIFA-direct for the specific match you care about. The cheapest source is not the same one twice in a row. It flips, sometimes within a single day, and the only way to catch it is to look at all of them at once instead of trusting whichever tab you happened to open.

A few things worth knowing as you go:

- FIFA refreshes inventory in batches, not continuously. Seats that vanished last week can reappear, often at the original face value, when someone's hold expires or a new release drops. Watching for that beats paying a resale premium out of impatience.
- "Official" does not mean "cheapest." FIFA's own variable pricing has pushed Category 1 seats well above what resellers are asking on the same match. Check both. Always.
- A price alert is worth more than a daily habit. The single most expensive mistake we see is checking three times a day, getting attached, and panic-buying at the top. Set your number, then walk away.

### Your final-week checklist

Run this once, today, and let it work while you get on with your life:

1. **Pick your match and your category.** Decide before you shop whether you're a buy-or-lose Cat-4 buyer or a hold-and-watch Cat-1/2 buyer. The plan is different for each.
2. **Pull up the match on TicketScan and compare all four prices** — FIFA-direct, Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, StubHub — side by side. Note which is cheapest right now and roughly by how much.
3. **Set a price alert at your real ceiling**, not a wishful one. For Cat-4, set it at or just above the current floor so you're notified if anything slips. For premium seats, set it below today's price and let resale come to you.
4. **Check the FIFA inventory drop midweek.** New batches tend to land before the weekend. A fresh release at face value can beat everything on resale.
5. **When your alert fires, buy.** That's the whole point of setting it. If the number you asked for shows up, don't second-guess it into a worse one.

The fans who do best in the next nine days aren't the ones with the deepest pockets. They're the ones who decided what they wanted, set a target, and let a tool do the watching instead of doing it emotionally at 1am.
