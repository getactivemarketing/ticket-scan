## Same game, same seats, a $478 swing

Knicks-Spurs Game 1 tips off tonight, June 3, at 8:30 ET in San Antonio. It is the first NBA Finals game in the Frost Bank Center since the Spurs and Knicks last met for a title in 1999, and the resale market has spent the last 48 hours doing something genuinely useful for once: disagreeing with itself.

Here is the cheapest get-in for the same game, pulled this morning across the platforms most fans actually check:

- GameTime: around $790
- SeatGeek: around $767, fees included
- Ticketmaster primary (balcony 200s): $969.21
- TickPick: $972
- Ticketmaster resale: $1,097 to $1,245

That is the same building, the same night, the same upper-bowl tier. The difference between the cheapest and most expensive front door is roughly $478 — about 62 percent — and the only thing separating them is which tab you happened to open first.

This is the part single-source sites can never tell you. Ticketmaster's app will quote you its own resale floor, north of $1,000, and it has no reason to mention that SeatGeek is sitting $300 lower on a comparable seat. Why would it? You are already on the page. The whole premium exists because most people check one source, see a number that feels "market," and pay it.

### Why San Antonio is the soft spot of the series

There is a second story buried in those numbers, and it cuts against the usual Finals panic. Prices are dropping.

The SeatGeek floor fell from about $1,000 on June 1 to $767 on June 2. That is a $233 drop, roughly 23 percent, in a single day, on the most-watched series in basketball. That does not happen when demand is overwhelming inventory. It happens when San Antonio is a smaller market than New York, the Spurs have plenty of resident season-ticket holders dumping seats they will not use, and the building is not MSG.

The series is reportedly priced cheapest at the San Antonio end. Games at Madison Square Garden later in the series are the expensive ones, because that is where the demand and the corporate money live. So if you have been telling yourself you will catch a later game in New York to "save money," the math probably runs the other way. Tonight, in Texas, is the value window — and it is open for a few more hours, not days.

A quick reality check on the top end, because the headlines love it: courtside at Frost Bank starts around $23,000 and runs past $42,000. That number is real, and it is also irrelevant to roughly everyone reading this. The interesting action is in the $700s, not the $40,000s.

## What to actually do before tip-off tonight

You do not need a system for this. You need to refuse to pay the first number you see.

1. Check more than one source. The whole point of tonight's spread is that the platforms are not agreeing. SeatGeek and GameTime are the floors right now; Ticketmaster resale is the ceiling. Pulling all of them side by side takes about 20 seconds and is worth $300.

2. Buy late, not early. Prices fell every day this week. For a game in a smaller market with soft demand, the seller's leverage shrinks as tip-off approaches and unsold inventory turns into a total loss. The two hours before the doors open are usually the cheapest of the entire window.

3. Sort by total price, fees included. SeatGeek and TickPick show the all-in number up front. Some platforms still tack the service fee on at checkout, which is exactly how a $767 ticket quietly becomes an $880 ticket. Compare the number you will actually pay, not the bait price.

4. Ignore the courtside numbers entirely. They are there to make the $900 seat feel reasonable by contrast. It is an old anchoring trick. The deal is in the upper bowl.

Game 1 is the one moment all season where the same seat is openly listed at five different prices across five apps, in real time, with the clock running. It is the cleanest possible argument for comparing before you buy. Tonight it is worth about $478.
