## The question everyone asks seven days out: "Can't I just buy from FIFA?"

It feels safe. FIFA is the official seller, the tickets are guaranteed real, and there's no scalper in the middle. So the instinct, with the 2026 World Cup now seven days from kickoff, is to open FIFA's platform, buy, and stop thinking about it.

Here's the problem with that instinct: **"official" and "cheapest" stopped being the same thing the moment FIFA dropped its price caps.**

For matches in the United States and Canada, FIFA abandoned the fixed-price model it used in previous tournaments. Prices on its own platform now move with demand — which means for the marquee matches they move up, and for the softer ones FIFA is often sitting on inventory it priced before reality set in. Meanwhile the resale market (SeatGeek, StubHub, Ticketmaster, Vivid Seats) is doing the opposite: real people trying to offload seats they can't use, and undercutting each other to do it.

The result, as of this week, is a market where the answer to "is FIFA cheaper?" is **sometimes — and you have to check.** Let's break it down by category.

## Category by category: where FIFA wins and where it loses

We pulled get-in (cheapest available) prices across FIFA-direct and the major resale platforms this week. The numbers shift hourly and by match, so treat these as the shape of the market, not a fixed quote. But the shape is the whole story.

| Ticket tier | FIFA-direct (official) | Resale floor (SeatGeek / StubHub) | Who's cheaper |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cat-4 group stage (the floor) | ~$80–$120, often unmoved | ~$100–$162, varies by match | **Usually FIFA** |
| Cat-3 / Cat-2 group stage | Repriced upward on demand | ~$200 seen on softer matches | **Often resale** |
| Soft group-stage matches (low demand) | Still at original list | As low as ~$100, whole blocks dumping | **Resale, clearly** |
| Cat-1 premium / knockout | Climbing toward the ceiling | Undercutting as sellers panic | **Resale, often** |
| The Final | $10,990 face | From ~$7,291 on StubHub resale | **Resale, by ~$3,700** |

Read that bottom row twice. The single most expensive ticket in the history of the sport — FIFA's $10,990 Category 1 final seat — is being undercut by its own resale market by nearly four thousand dollars. That is the entire thesis in one line: **once a primary seller goes fully dynamic, the secondary market frequently becomes the cheaper option.**

### So when *is* FIFA-direct actually the move?

Two situations:

- **The Cat-4 floor seat that never moved.** For lower-demand matches, FIFA's cheapest official ticket was often set early and left alone. Resale can't reliably undercut a $90 official seat, because the seller's own cost plus fees floors them out. If you want the genuine cheapest get-in on a non-glamour group-stage match, FIFA-direct is frequently it.
- **You value the guarantee over the last $20.** FIFA-direct tickets transfer through the official app with zero counterparty risk. For some buyers that certainty is worth paying a small premium. That's a real reason — just know you're paying for peace of mind, not for the lowest price.

Everywhere else — premium seats, knockout matches, and any match where demand came in soft — resale is now the value play. The "official is safest so it must be fairest" assumption is costing fans real money this week.

## The signal that tells you the floor is about to drop

Here's a tactical edge most buyers miss. This week, reporting surfaced **large contiguous blocks** — entire rows across multiple sections — appearing on SeatGeek for softer group-stage matches, including Saudi Arabia–Cape Verde in Houston on June 26.

When whole blocks hit resale at once, that's not random. It's a brokerage or a group buyer dumping inventory they couldn't move at the price they hoped for. It is a leading indicator that a match is over-supplied and its floor is about to fall further. The consumer-advocate read:

> **Whole-block availability on a non-marquee match = a wait-and-watch signal, not a buy signal.** If you can see ten rows of get-in seats, you are not the desperate one in this transaction. The seller is.

The matches where you should move fast are the inverse: thin inventory, a single demand-heavy fixture (a host nation playing, a Group of Death matchup), and a FIFA-direct floor that hasn't budged in weeks. Those don't get cheaper. The over-supplied ones do.
