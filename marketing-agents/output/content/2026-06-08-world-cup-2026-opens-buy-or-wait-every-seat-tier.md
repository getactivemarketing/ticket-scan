## There are two World Cups happening, and they have nothing to do with each other

The 2026 World Cup opens Thursday, June 11. With four days left before the first whistle, the ticket market has split cleanly in two, and which half you're shopping in decides whether you should buy tonight or sit on your hands.

In one half, the cheap seats are in free fall. Goal.com reports that "panic-selling" has dragged standard group-stage resale down to neutral fixtures starting around **$116 to $120**: Ivory Coast versus Curacao opened around $116, Qatar versus Switzerland around $120, Czech Republic versus South Africa about the same. A month ago those same categories carried far heavier price tags.

In the other half, nothing has budged. The marquee and knockout matches are, in Goal's words, "holding securely in the stratosphere." ESPN reported FIFA tripled the best-available final ticket to **$33,000**. Category 1 semifinal and final face value runs roughly **$1,500 to $6,730** before you ever touch resale.

Same tournament. Same week. A 280x spread from the floor to the ceiling. So the honest answer to "should I buy now or wait?" isn't one answer. It's five, one per tier. Here's the call on each.

## The framework: have these seats already bottomed, or do they still have air to fall?

Every buy-or-wait decision comes down to a single question. Is the price you're looking at already near the floor, or is there room below it? Seats that have bottomed don't reward patience; you wait, inventory thins, and you pay more out of FOMO. Seats with air still under them punish the impatient. The trick is knowing which tier you're in.

### Tier 1 — Cat-4 and softer group-stage seats: **BUY**

This is where panic-selling lives. Brokers and group buyers who overpaid in the lottery phase are now dumping inventory before it becomes worthless on Thursday, and that's why neutral fixtures are starting near $116. A ticket that has fallen this far, this close to kickoff, is not getting meaningfully cheaper. Once the match is 48 hours out, the supply of nervous sellers dries up and the floor firms.

If you have a group-stage match you actually want to attend in a non-glamour city, this is your green light. Compare every platform first, because at these prices the gap between the cheapest and second-cheapest source can be 20% of the ticket, but stop waiting for a better number. You're already standing on it.

### Tier 2 — Mid-tier group stage (Cat-2 / Cat-3): **BUY SELECTIVELY**

Goal.com pegs Cat-3 group-stage face value at $100 to $200 and Cat-2 at $150 to $280. These are softening too, but less violently than the floor. The play here is conditional. If the match features two neutral nations and sits at the top of that range, give it another day; over-supplied mid-tier seats are still drifting down. If it's a host nation or a popular side and inventory looks thin, treat it like Tier 1 and buy. The tell is volume: lots of get-in listings means a soft match with room to fall, a handful of listings means demand that won't wait for you.

### Tier 3 — Marquee group-stage matches (USA, Mexico, England, Brazil): **WAIT, BARELY**

Host-nation and blue-blood group games are the trap tier. They feel urgent, so people overpay early. But the genuinely scarce inventory doesn't free up until the final 72 hours, when ticket holders give up on selling at a dream price and relist to recover something. If your match is in this group and you're not traveling on a fixed date, set a price alert and let the late relisting wave come to you. Just know the window is narrow and closes hard at kickoff.

### Tier 4 — Knockout rounds (Round of 32 through quarters): **WAIT**

You can't even know who's playing yet, and that's the entire reason to wait. Knockout face value runs $150 to $1,200 depending on category and round per Goal's breakdown, but resale won't find its real level until the bracket fills in and casual buyers learn their team is out. Buying a knockout seat today is buying a lottery ticket on a matchup that doesn't exist. Track the venue, not the match, and pounce once the fixtures are set.

### Tier 5 — Semifinals and the Final: **SKIP (or accept you're paying the FOMO tax)**

There is no value play at the top. A $33,000 best-available final ticket isn't priced on supply and demand; it's priced on the fact that someone, somewhere, will pay it. Cat-1 final face value already sits up to $6,730 before fees. If attending the final is a bucket-list line item and money is genuinely no object, buy it and enjoy it, with our blessing. But understand you are not getting a deal, you are paying a number FIFA chose because it could. For everyone else, the semifinals on a TV with friends is the smart-money seat.

## The one rule that survives all five tiers

Whatever tier you land in, the mechanics are identical: never buy from the first tab you opened. FIFA's resale marketplace is the only authorized face-value platform, and it charges a 15% buyer fee plus a 15% seller fee, so "official" rarely means "cheapest."
