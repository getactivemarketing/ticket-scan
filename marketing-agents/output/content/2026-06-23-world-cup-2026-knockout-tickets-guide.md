## The group stage is closing. The knockout scramble is starting.

The 2026 World Cup group stage wraps around June 27, and the bracket is filling in match by match. That is the exact moment ticket prices stop being predictable. Group-stage seats were a known quantity — fixed slots, fixed cities, weeks of lead time. The knockout rounds are the opposite: you often do not know who is playing until a few days before kickoff, the matchups swing wildly in demand, and every marketplace reprices on the fly.

That unpredictability is also where the deals live, if you know how to read it. On the eve of the tournament, 75 games still had tickets available, including both semifinals, all four quarterfinals, and five Round of 16 matches (per ABC News). Inventory is not the problem. Overpaying out of panic is.

Here is the playbook.

## First, understand what just happened to prices

FIFA raised the top price of the final at MetLife Stadium to a record **$32,970** for a Front Category 1 seat, with Category 4 still listed around $2,030 (ABC News, ESPN). On the resale market, the cheapest get-in for the final was hovering near **$8,303** in mid-June. Those are eye-watering numbers, and they are doing exactly what a headline price is designed to do: anchor your expectations high so everything else feels reasonable by comparison.

Do not let the final's sticker price set your mental baseline for a Round of 16 game in Kansas City or Philadelphia. Those are completely different markets. The cheapest neutral matches were still landing in the $200–$250 range around kickoff. The knockout rounds sit somewhere in between, and where exactly depends almost entirely on which two teams draw into your match.

## The myth that costs fans the most: "official is always cheapest"

This is the single most expensive assumption in tournament ticketing, and 2026 is proving it wrong in real time.

Reporting from mid-June found multiple matches where FIFA's own resale platform was *more* expensive than the secondary market — and several where it was *cheaper*. For the Paraguay–US match, resale tickets started around **$661 on FIFA** versus about **$920 on SeatGeek**. For England against Croatia, it was roughly **$850 on FIFA** versus **$880 on SeatGeek** (ABC News). The cheapest source flipped depending on the match, the day, and the seat category.

The lesson is not "FIFA is cheaper" or "resale is cheaper." It is that there is no permanent winner. The price leadership changes constantly, which means the only way to actually pay the lowest price is to look at every source at once, every time.

## Watch the fine print: the all-in number is what matters

Sticker price is a trap if you stop reading there. FIFA's resale marketplace adds fees on both sides of the transaction — commonly cited as **15% for the buyer plus 15% for the seller**, which can push the total markup toward 30% per transaction (per FIFA's support documentation, corroborated by Goal.com). A listing that looks $40 cheaper on one platform can quietly become $60 more expensive once fees, delivery charges, and currency conversion land on the final screen.

When you compare knockout tickets, compare the **all-in checkout total**, not the headline number. That one habit beats almost every "insider" buying tip out there.

## Your knockout buying checklist

### 1. Wait for the matchup to set — then move fast
For Round of 16 and beyond, you frequently will not know both teams until a couple of days out. Prices for a generic "Round of 16, Seat at SoFi" placeholder can sit flat, then spike or crater the moment the bracket locks and fans realize whether it is a marquee clash or a low-demand pairing. If you can stay flexible on which match you attend, you hold the advantage.

### 2. Compare all three marketplaces side by side
Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, and StubHub each have different inventory and different fee structures, and FIFA's official resale is a fourth variable on top. Pull the match up and look at all of them together. This is the entire reason TicketScan exists — a single side-by-side view so you are not flipping between five tabs while prices move under you.

### 3. Set a target price and let the alert do the work
The fans who overpay are the ones refreshing prices ten times a day and eventually cracking. Decide what the seat is worth to you, set a price alert at that number, and close the tab. When any marketplace drops below your target, you get an email. No emotion, no panic-buy at the top.

### 4. Favor the lower-demand host cities
Some of the best knockout value sits in host cities where corporate buyers are not camping the resale market. A Round of 16 match in a non-glamour US city can cost a fraction of the same round at a marquee venue. Travel costs matter, but the ticket math often more than makes up for it.

### 5. Know your walk-away number before you look
Set your ceiling before the matchup is announced, while you are still thinking clearly. Once your team is in the bracket, every price will feel justifiable. The number you wrote down yesterday is more honest than the one your gut will offer you on game week.

## The bottom line

The knockout rounds reward information and patience, not the biggest budget. Prices are volatile, official is not automatically cheapest, and fees can erase a "deal" at the last screen. Compare every source, compare the all-in total, set an alert, and refuse to let the final's $32,970 headline talk you into overpaying for a Round of 16 seat.

The tournament is the best month of soccer on earth. Pay the right price to be there.
