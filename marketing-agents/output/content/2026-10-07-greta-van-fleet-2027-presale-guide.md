## Greta Van Fleet's presale calendar is a small administrative obstacle course

Greta Van Fleet's 2027 *Into The Beginning* tour is here, and the ticket calendar is doing what ticket calendars do best: making a perfectly normal concert feel like a timed exam.

The important dates depend on where you want to see the band. European fan presales begin Wednesday, October 7, at 10 a.m. local time. Additional presales follow, and the European general onsale begins Friday, October 9, at 10 a.m. local time. For North American dates, the fan presale begins Tuesday, October 13, at 9 a.m. local time, with general onsale Thursday, October 15, at 10 a.m. local time.

Those details come from the [Live Nation tour announcement](https://news.livenationentertainment.com/news/greta-van-fleet-announce-into-the-beginning-2027-north-american-and-european-tour). Individual event pages can show different access rules, so treat the announcement as the map and the event page as the final instruction sheet.

## What is happening with the Into The Beginning tour?

The tour supports Greta Van Fleet's forthcoming album *Palace for the People*. The announced routing includes North American arena dates beginning in Savannah in February 2027, European dates in May and June, and Canadian dates in July before the tour wraps in Grand Rapids, Michigan, on July 31.

The North American schedule includes stops in cities such as Atlanta, Chicago, Detroit, Boston, Brooklyn, Philadelphia, Anaheim, Phoenix, Seattle, Denver, Toronto, and Grand Rapids. European dates include Munich, Milan, Hamburg, Copenhagen, Berlin, Prague, Amsterdam, Brussels, Paris, London, Birmingham, Manchester, Glasgow, and Dublin.

That is a lot of dates, which is good news if you have some flexibility. It also creates a new problem: the “right” ticket page is not one universal page. Each venue and country may use its own ticketing partner, presale eligibility, local time zone, ticket limit, and delivery rules.

## The five things to verify before the presale opens

### 1. Your date and city

Start with the exact event, not a broad search for “Greta Van Fleet tickets.” A tour can have multiple nearby dates, different venues, and separate ticket pages. Confirm the city, venue, date, and doors or start time before you enter a queue.

TicketScan is useful here because it helps surface the event and its onsale information in one place. Once you click through, check the official event page again. A queue is not a substitute for reading the label at the top of the page.

### 2. The time zone

“10 a.m. local time” means 10 a.m. where the venue is located, not necessarily 10 a.m. where you are sitting with three browser tabs open and a very optimistic beverage.

Ticketmaster event pages for European dates show the local start times and may use different abbreviations, such as CEST or BST. Convert the time before the sale, then set a reminder for a few minutes earlier. The [Munich event page](https://www.ticketmaster.de/event/greta-van-fleet-into-the-beginning-tickets/1124094012?language=en-us), for example, lists presale activity beginning at 10 a.m. CEST on October 7.

### 3. Your presale eligibility

The tour announcement says fans can sign up for the Greta Van Fleet fan presale through the band's official site. Some event pages also list album, artist, Mastercard, or partner presales. Those are separate access paths, not one giant presale with several decorative labels.

Read the requirement for the specific window you want. You may need an invitation, a code, an eligible payment card, an artist-store purchase, or an account with the ticketing partner. If the page does not say you are eligible, assume you still need to confirm—not that the queue will sort it out for you.

### 4. The ticket limit and delivery method

Ticket limits can apply per person, account, household, email address, payment card, or other linked information. The Munich event page, for example, lists a limit of six tickets per person and per card. Limits vary by event, so do not rely on a screenshot from a different city.

Also check whether tickets are mobile-only, whether screenshots are accepted, and whether transfer restrictions apply. The ticket is not fully understood until you know how it gets into your phone and through the venue door.

### 5. The final checkout terms

A presale label does not tell you whether a listing is the right fit. Before paying, confirm the section, row, quantity, delivery method, seller, and all-in checkout total. Make sure the event name and date have not changed while you were clicking through.

This is where comparing sources helps. A single seller may be the official place to buy a particular primary ticket, but a ticket comparison tool can help you find the correct event page and see where the event is listed. Compare the actual terms attached to the exact seat or listing—not just the large number shown first on a seat map.

## What if you miss the fan presale?

Do not panic-buy from the first social post promising a “secret” link. Check the official artist page, venue page, and ticketing page for the next legitimate presale or general onsale. For Greta Van Fleet's European dates, Live Nation says the general onsale begins October 9. For North American dates, it begins October 15.

If you are considering a resale listing, inspect the seller, ticket location, delivery timing, transfer rules, fees, and refund policy before checkout. Beware of speculative listings that do not clearly explain whether the seller already has the ticket. A countdown timer is not proof of legitimacy; it is just a countdown timer wearing a tiny hat.

## The practical takeaway

For this tour, the best preparation is boring and specific: pick the exact date, convert the local onsale time, confirm the presale requirement, check the ticket limit, and read the delivery and checkout terms. That checklist is more useful than collecting ten questionable codes from strangers online.
