## Why this exists

The 2026 World Cup runs 39 days across 16 stadiums in three countries. For most matches, the cheapest, safest moment to buy is **inside the final 48 hours** — when sellers who guessed wrong start cutting prices and panic-selling kicks in. But that same window is where the avoidable mistakes happen: paying a "Front Category" markup for a seat you didn't realize was repriced, buying a transfer ticket that never arrives, or eating a 30%+ platform gap because you only checked one site.

This checklist walks you through the four phases of a matchday purchase. Work top to bottom. If you can't tick a box, that's your signal to slow down.

---

## Phase 1 — Before you search (24–48 hours out)

- [ ] **Decide which "World Cup" you're shopping in.** Neutral group-stage matches between non-host nations tend to soften as kickoff nears (panic-selling). Host-nation matches, marquee draws, and any knockout game hold firm or climb. Know which one your match is before you set expectations.
- [ ] **Set your walk-away number now, while you're calm.** Write down the most you'll pay, all-in (ticket + fees). The final-48 window is designed to make you abandon it. Don't.
- [ ] **Check the official face value first.** Look up the FIFA Category 1–4 face price for your match so you have a baseline. If resale is *below* face, that's a real signal the panic-sell has started. If it's 3× face, you're paying a demand premium — decide if it's worth it.
- [ ] **Confirm the section actually matters to you.** "Category 1" is not one ring of seats — corners behind the goal and sideline-midfield can both be Cat 1 at very different real-world quality. Look at a seat map before you fall in love with a category number.
- [ ] **Set a price alert instead of refreshing tabs.** Track the match across platforms and let the drop come to you. (TicketScan does this in one search.)

## Phase 2 — Before you buy (compare, every time)

- [ ] **Compare the *same seat* across at least three platforms.** Ticketmaster/official, SeatGeek, and StubHub routinely price the identical section differently — gaps of 20–40% on the same listing are normal, not rare.
- [ ] **Compare the FIFA resale marketplace against secondary platforms.** "Official" is not automatically cheapest. For some group-stage games, FIFA's own resale marketplace undercuts secondary; for others it's the reverse. Check both.
- [ ] **Read the *all-in* price, not the sticker.** Add service fees, delivery fees, and any currency conversion before you compare. The cheapest sticker is frequently not the cheapest checkout.
- [ ] **For cross-border matches (Canada / Mexico venues), check the currency.** A CAD or MXN listing converts to a different USD total than it looks. FX swings can quietly move your effective price 5–10%.
- [ ] **Beware "upgraded" tiers that didn't exist at launch.** If you see a brand-new premium category carved out of seats that used to be standard Cat 1, you may be paying extra for a sideline view that original buyers already thought they'd purchased. Know what you're actually buying.

## Phase 3 — Before you pay (don't get played)

- [ ] **Confirm the delivery method and timing in writing.** Mobile transfer is standard for the World Cup. Make sure the listing states *when* the transfer arrives — "by matchday" is not the same as "now."
- [ ] **Verify the platform's buyer guarantee.** Reputable secondary platforms guarantee valid entry or your money back. Screenshot the guarantee terms before you pay.
- [ ] **Never pay by bank transfer, gift card, or cash app to a stranger.** No legitimate resale runs that way. If a "fan" in a Facebook group or DM insists on it, walk away — that's the single most common World Cup ticket scam.
- [ ] **Match the name/transfer requirements.** Some tickets require the buyer's FIFA ID or name on transfer. If yours does, confirm you can meet it *before* paying, or the ticket is worthless at the gate.
- [ ] **Re-check your walk-away number one last time.** If the all-in total crept past the number from Phase 1, close the tab. There's another matchday tomorrow.

## Phase 4 — Before you travel (matchday)

- [ ] **Confirm the ticket is actually in your wallet/app** — not "pending transfer." Don't leave for the stadium on a promise.
- [ ] **Screenshot the barcode/entry pass** as a backup in case of dead signal at the venue.
- [ ] **Plan transit, not just the seat.** The cheapest get-in at a far-flung stadium can cost more once you add parking or a rideshare surge. Some venues are a cheap train ride from downtown; others aren't. Factor it in.
- [ ] **Know the stadium's bag/clear-bag and entry-time policy** before you arrive — World Cup security lines are long; late arrival can mean missing the opening.
- [ ] **Keep your alert running until you walk in.** If you haven't bought yet and you're flexible and local, the steepest drops happen in the last few hours before kickoff. Patience is the last edge.
