## Thursday Focus: Event detail `/event/[id]` re-audit

The Event detail page is the **richest content surface on the platform** — it renders the 30-day price history chart, the buy/wait/hold recommendation, the price trend indicator, the tier-by-section price estimates (upper bowl / lower bowl / club / floor / suite), and a 10-row recent-price-checks table. This is the page that *justifies* the entire value prop of TicketScan. **It is also the single most-locked-down surface on the platform.** Three structural failures compound:

1. **Hard auth gate at `:108–112`** (Day 42) — anonymous visitors are redirected to `/login` before any content renders. The redirect is unconditional and synchronous; it does not even attempt to render a teaser or a meta description.
2. **No `layout.tsx`** (confirmed today via `ls`) — combined with the `'use client'` directive at `:1`, no `metadata` or `generateMetadata` can be exported. The page renders to crawlers as: redirect to `/login`. **Zero of the 110 watchlist event IDs are indexable.**
3. **No public-facing entrypoint** — the only link to `/event/[id]` on the entire frontend is the `<Link href={`/event/${event.id}`}>` in the watchlist page's row (gated). External shares of an `/event/[id]` URL would land any non-logged-in user on the login page, with no fallback to (a) tell them what they're missing or (b) preserve the URL for after-login resolution.

The combined effect: **the platform's deepest content asset is invisible to every visitor who has not already created an account and added the event to their watchlist.** The page is the inverse of a discovery surface — it is a retention surface that retroactively explains why a logged-in user should care about a watchlist item, but it does no acquisition work.
