## Page Review — Venue template (`web/src/app/venues/[slug]/page.tsx`)

This is a cold-traffic SEO landing page (visitors arrive from Google). Three real, code-grounded friction points:

**P1 — Every conversion path forces account creation; no low-friction first step.**
All four CTAs route anon visitors straight to `/register`: "Track Price" (`:217`), "Sign up to get notified" (`:231`), sidebar "Start Free" (`:287`). The only no-account capture on the page is the footer `NewsletterSignup`, which is below the fold. A cold visitor who isn't ready to make an account has nothing to do in the main column or sidebar.
→ **Recommendation:** Add an inline `NewsletterSignup variant="inline"` to the sidebar CTA box (or directly under the events list), framed as "Get price-drop alerts for {venue.name} — no account needed." Captures email at the visitor's actual intent level. Matches the newsletter-capture pattern already shipped on blog/footer. *(Dev backlog — small, additive.)*

**P2 — No value proof above the fold.**
The hero (`:148–177`) states only the venue name, city, and capacity. There's no statement of what TicketScan does or why to trust it — no "compare Ticketmaster, SeatGeek & StubHub in one place," no proof point. Value prop fails the 5-second test on the exact pages where cold traffic lands.
→ **Recommendation:** Add a one-line trust strip under the H1: "Compare prices across Ticketmaster, SeatGeek & StubHub — free." Zero data dependency; pure copy. *(→ Content Agent / dev backlog.)*

**P3 — Seating tiers show "~1.8x base" with no base price.**
The seating sidebar (`:258–271`) renders multipliers like "~1.8x base" — meaningless without a base dollar figure. Reads as confusing filler to a first-time visitor.
→ **Recommendation:** Either show illustrative ranges (even "$$ / $$$") or drop the multiplier column. Low priority, but it's confusion not persuasion.
