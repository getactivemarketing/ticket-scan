## Homepage Review

### What is working

- Primary value proposition is visible above the fold: “Compare. Track. Save.” plus real-time pricing and price-drop alerts.
- The hero has a clear primary CTA: **Get Started Free**, with a lower-friction secondary path: **See How It Works**.
- The page offers multiple conversion paths: registration, event discovery, World Cup venue exploration, newsletter signup, and a final account CTA.
- The newsletter card has one field, responsive stacking, a loading state, success state, and “No spam, unsubscribe anytime” reassurance.

### Recommendations

1. **P0 — Instrument the homepage funnel before optimization.** Add `dataLayer.push` events for `cta_click`, `search_initiated`, `signup_completed`, `newsletter_subscribed`, and `watchlist_added`; include route, CTA/form variant, event ID where relevant, and no PII. Add UTM persistence through signup/newsletter completion. Without this, CRO is flying by dashboard-shaped vibes.

2. **P1 — Make the first action lower-friction and more product-specific.** Change the hero CTA from “Get Started Free” to **Compare Ticket Prices** and link it to `/dashboard`; retain a secondary **Create Free Price Alert** path to `/register`. This aligns the click promise with the product’s strongest immediate value and avoids asking users to register before they have seen a result.

3. **P1 — Replace or substantiate generic social proof.** The homepage displays “50K+ Events Tracked,” “$127 Avg. Savings,” “5+ Sites Compared,” and “Join thousands of fans,” but the available admin data does not substantiate those claims. Use verifiable live totals or rewrite to concrete product facts such as “Compare Ticketmaster and SeatGeek in one search.”

4. **P2 — Bring proof closer to the hero CTA.** Add one compact, verifiable proof point near the hero (for example, “See estimated fees before you click out”) and a short example of a price comparison. Current proof appears below the fold in the stats bar and static trending cards.

### Mobile and performance checks

- CTA and form controls use comfortable padding and responsive stacks; the homepage should be browser-tested at 320px and 375px widths because the hero and stats bar use large type and fixed-ish spacing.
- No reliable live performance measurement was available in the supplied analytics. Run a mobile Lighthouse/Web Vitals check after deployment; treat LCP over 2.5s or interaction delay over 200ms as follow-up items.
- Static “Trending Deals” content should either show a freshness timestamp or be clearly labeled as examples; dated/stale-looking prices can undermine trust.

## Newsletter Form Review

### Findings

- Field count is good: one required email field.
- `type="email"` is correctly used, so mobile email keyboards should appear.
- The field has no visible `<label>`; the placeholder is doing label work and disappears while typing.
- Button copy is generic in the card/footer variants (`Subscribe`); the inline variant uses stronger “Get Deals” copy.
- Loading, success, and error states exist. Errors are rendered as plain text but are not visibly announced with `role="alert"` or `aria-live`.
- The component clears the email after success and does not expose a confirmation event to analytics.

### Form recommendation — P1

Standardize the promise and accessibility across variants:

```text
Label: Email address
Supporting copy: Daily ticket deals, price-drop alerts, and buying tips. No spam.
Button: Send me ticket deals
Loading: Sending…
Success: You’re on the list — watch your inbox for the next deal.
Error: We couldn’t subscribe you. Check the email and try again.
```

Implementation spec: add a visually-hidden label, `autoComplete="email"`, `inputMode="email"`, `aria-live="polite"` for success, `role="alert"` for errors, and a `newsletter_subscribed` event only after the API returns success. Keep the one-field form and responsive layout.
