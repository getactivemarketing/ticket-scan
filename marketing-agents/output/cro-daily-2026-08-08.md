### Page-Level Conversion Review — World Cup hub

**What works**

- Primary CTA is visible above the fold and links directly to a prefilled dashboard search.
- Value proposition is understandable within five seconds: compare actual prices across Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, and StubHub.
- Mobile CTA layout stacks vertically on small screens, and the hero uses a responsive two-column-to-four-column stats grid.
- Live response checks returned HTTP 200; measured request totals were approximately 0.31s for the World Cup page, 0.30s for the blog, and 0.48s for the homepage. These are server/request timings, not Core Web Vitals.

**Recommendations**

1. **P0 — Align the hero with post-event intent.** Replace “Explore WC 2026 Prices” with a current promise such as “See What World Cup Tickets Cost” and add a secondary CTA, “Get Ticket Deals for Future Matches,” routed to the newsletter or current-events search. Update the nav/footer label from “World Cup 2026” to “World Cup Price Guide” if the route remains live.
2. **P1 — Add a contextual email capture before the long venue grid.** Place a compact World Cup-specific signup after the hero stats: “Get the next ticket-price drop in your inbox.” Use `source="world-cup-hub"`; keep the global footer capture as a secondary path.
3. **P1 — Add proof tied to the product.** Replace generic “48 / 104 / 16 / 3” facts with one measurable product proof point when available, such as comparisons completed, price snapshots tracked, or median cross-market spread. Do not use invented social proof.

### Blog → Newsletter Conversion Review

- The blog has a clear informational value proposition and a newsletter card at the bottom of the index.
- The newsletter CTA is late in the page and competes with the article list; there is no visible signup near the hero or between featured content and all articles.
- Blog posts use a footer-style signup, which is compact but less persuasive and still uses “Subscribe.”
- Recommendation: add one inline, context-specific CTA after the featured articles: “Get the next ticket-buying edge before you need it.” Keep one email field and use `source="blog-featured-cta"` for attribution.

### Form Friction Check — Newsletter signup

**Current state**

- One required field; good minimum friction.
- Correct `type="email"` and browser validation are present.
- No visible `<label>`; the placeholder carries the field instruction and disappears while typing.
- Footer button copy is generic (“Subscribe”); loading state is only “...”, which is poor feedback and not accessible context.
- Error and success text are rendered, but no explicit `aria-live` region or server-side field-level guidance is visible in the component.

**Form recommendation — P1**

Add a visually hidden label, `aria-describedby` for the privacy/no-spam note, and `aria-live="polite"` around status messages. Change the footer CTA to “Get Ticket Deals” and loading copy to “Saving…” or “Joining…”. Preserve the single-field form. Add a lightweight inline error for invalid email and emit `newsletter_view`, `newsletter_start`, `newsletter_submit`, and `newsletter_success` events with the `source` value.
