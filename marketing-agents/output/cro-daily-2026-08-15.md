### Page-Level Conversion Review — Blog / Newsletter Path

**What works**

- The blog hero explains the benefit quickly: ticket deals, when to buy, price comparison, and saving money.
- The blog index has a prominent newsletter card after the article list, and every page also includes a footer capture.
- Blog posts include related articles, quick links to Search Events and Compare Prices, and a sidebar newsletter form. This creates several relevant next steps.
- The site is responsive in source layout: article content and sidebar collapse at the `lg` breakpoint, and newsletter forms stack on small screens.

**Recommendations**

1. **P1 — Use a comparison-first CTA in article CTAs.** Change “Create Free Account” to “Compare Ticket Prices” linking to `/compare`, with “Set a Price Alert” as the secondary action. If `/compare` still requires authentication, preserve the article context/query and explain the account requirement rather than silently bouncing the reader to registration.
2. **P1 — Move a compact newsletter capture closer to article intent.** Add a short inline block after the first high-value section or around 50% scroll: “Get the price drop, not the panic-buy.” Keep the existing end-of-article/sidebar capture as the second opportunity. Measure view → start → success by placement.
3. **P2 — Make the newsletter offer concrete.** Replace broad “exclusive deals and price drop alerts” language with “1–2 emails/week: price drops, fee math, and the best time to buy.” Add a dated sample or link to a recent issue so the value is verifiable.

### Form Friction Check — Newsletter Signup

**Current state:** The form has one required `type="email"` field, which is appropriately low friction and triggers the email keyboard on mobile. Blog index uses the card variant; blog posts use a footer-style sidebar form; the global footer appears on every page. There is no persistent visible label, `aria-live` status region, popup, or source-level conversion event. The footer loading button displays `...`; card/inline variants say “Subscribing…”. Failed submissions retain the entered email, which is good.

**Form improvement specification**

- **P1:** Add a visually hidden label plus `aria-describedby` for a short expectation/privacy note; do not rely on the placeholder as the field name.
- **P1:** Use action-oriented copy consistently: “Send me ticket deals” for the card and “Get deals” for the footer/sidebar. Replace `...` with “Signing up…”.
- **P1:** Add `aria-live="polite"` to success/error feedback and distinguish invalid email from API failure with recoverable messages.
- **P2:** State cadence explicitly: “1–2 emails/week · no spam · unsubscribe anytime.”
- **P1:** Emit `newsletter_view`, `newsletter_start`, `newsletter_submit`, `newsletter_success`, and `newsletter_error` with `source` (`blog-index`, `blog-post`, `site-footer`), placement, and error category.
- **P2:** Do not add a popup until inline/sidebar performance is measurable. If added, frequency-cap it and measure exposure → start → success separately.
