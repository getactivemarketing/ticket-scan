## World Cup Page Review

### What works

- **Fast and available:** deployed page returned HTTP 200; measured total response time was ~0.34s and HTML was ~105 KB.
- **Clear above-the-fold hierarchy:** event status, title, dates, four concrete stats, and two CTAs are visible in the initial HTML.
- **Strong internal discovery structure:** schedule, country sections, stadium guide links, and “Find Tickets” links give visitors several relevant next steps.
- **Mobile-safe structure in source:** hero CTAs stack vertically on small screens; schedule controls use horizontal overflow for dates; match cards stack before the ticket CTA.

### Recommendations

1. **P0 — Replace the primary hero CTA for the post-event state.** Use “Explore Host Stadium Guides” → `#venues` or a dedicated `/world-cup-2026/venues` anchor. Keep “Review the Match Schedule” as the secondary action. This removes the dead-end risk from “Browse WC 2026 Events.”
2. **P1 — Add a compact post-event value proposition immediately below the hero.** Lead with the assets that remain useful: all 16 host-stadium guides, seating sections, capacities, and documented event/onsale information for future events. Do not mention TicketScan price history, alerts, or recommendations.
3. **P1 — Make each venue card’s action more specific.** Change generic “View Guide” to “See seating guide” and preserve the capacity fact. This makes the click intent clearer and gives the page a useful, measurable CTA family once events are instrumented.

### Copy request to Content Agent

Draft a replacement hero and post-event intro around: “The tournament is over; the stadium knowledge is still useful.” Include the 16 host stadiums, seating/capacity guidance, and links to future events. Avoid any claim that TicketScan tracks prices, sends price-drop alerts, or predicts when to buy.
