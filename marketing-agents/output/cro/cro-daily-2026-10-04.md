### Page-Level Conversion Review: Blog → Newsletter

**Priority 1 — Make the newsletter benefit more specific and supportable.** The blog index has a strong hero and a newsletter CTA after the article list (`NewsletterSignup source="blog-index"`), but the page headline promises “best ticket deals,” and the CTA is below a potentially long list of articles. Test a short supporting line near the hero or after Featured Articles: **“Get verified onsale and presale dates, venue guides, and practical ticket-buying tips.”** Keep the CTA focused on onsale/presale updates and education.

**Priority 1 — Add a nearer conversion bridge.** Add an inline newsletter CTA after Featured Articles or after the first two article cards, while retaining the full CTA at the bottom. This gives readers who have found a relevant article a next step without requiring a long scroll. Measure `newsletter_view` and `newsletter_submit` by placement before choosing a winner.

**Priority 2 — Remove unsupported or ambiguous pricing language from the path.** The blog hero says “best ticket deals” and “how to compare prices,” while the site footer says “Compare ticket prices.” Reframe to “ticket-buying tips,” “onsale dates,” “venue guides,” and general fee-checking advice until current pricing functionality is restored. The distinction matters: useful buying guidance is still true; TicketScan price-data claims are not.

Checklist:

- Primary CTA above the fold: **Needs work** — blog navigation and article discovery are above the fold, but the newsletter CTA is below the article list.
- Value proposition within five seconds: **Partial** — “Ticket Buying Tips & Guides” is clear; “best ticket deals” is too broad and can imply unavailable product data.
- Friction: **Partial** — content discovery is low friction, but the first email conversion opportunity arrives late in the page.
- Social proof: **Needs work** — no verified subscriber count, testimonials, or newsletter preview is shown. Do not add unverified numbers.
- Performance: **Pass for server response** — deployed `/blog` returned HTTP 200 in about 0.30 seconds. This is not a Core Web Vitals measurement.
- Mobile: **Likely pass from responsive source** — article cards stack on small screens and the newsletter form uses a responsive column layout; verify the CTA position and thumb reach on a real phone.
