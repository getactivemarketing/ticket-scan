## Cover copy

### The Ticket Buyer’s Cheat Sheet

The five-minute checklist for finding the right event, surviving the sale window, and checking the real total before you buy.

**Keep this open when tickets go on sale.**

## Page 1 — Before the sale

### 1. Confirm the event

- Artist or team
- City and venue
- Exact date and start time
- Whether the game or performance is guaranteed or conditional
- Local time zone for the sale

### 2. Identify the sale window

- Artist or fan-club presale
- Venue or promoter presale
- Cardholder or partner presale
- General public onsale
- Resale availability after the primary sale

Access to a presale is an opportunity to shop, not a guarantee of a ticket, seat, or price. Use the official artist, team, venue, promoter, or ticketing link to verify the rules.

### 3. Prepare your account

- Sign in before the waiting room opens.
- Confirm your email, phone, billing, and delivery details.
- Read the ticket limit.
- Keep the eligible card or access method available if the sale requires one.
- Use one device and browser unless the official instructions say otherwise.

The cheapest headline number is not a useful comparison if the date, seat location, quantity, or delivery terms are different.

### 5. Check the venue before you commit

- Is the section on the correct side of the building?
- Is the view marked obstructed or limited?
- Is the entrance tied to a particular gate or club level?
- Are there bag, mobile-ticket, age, accessibility, or transfer rules?
- Will you need public transit or an event-specific parking plan?

## Page 3 — Before checkout

### 6. Read the complete total

Check the total for the full quantity of tickets, including mandatory fees, delivery charges, taxes, and any required add-ons shown at checkout. Pricing displays differ by marketplace and event; the final order summary is the number to evaluate.

### 7. Check the protection and delivery terms

Confirm when tickets will arrive, how they will be transferred, what happens if the event changes, and what buyer protection applies. Do not pay a person through an unprotected channel because a listing looks urgent.

### 8. Set a personal stop rule

Before the queue or countdown starts, write down:

- Maximum total spend
- Minimum acceptable section or view
- Number of seats required
- Latest acceptable purchase time
- Backup date, venue, or event

This is a planning tool, not a prediction about future ticket prices. It keeps a fast sale from making the decision for you.
