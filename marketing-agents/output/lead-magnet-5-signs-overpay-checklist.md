### Sign 1 — You're only checking one platform

☐ **You opened Ticketmaster (or StubHub, or SeatGeek) and you haven't checked the other two yet.**

The same lower-bowl seat to the same event shows **20–50% price variance** across platforms on the same day. Pollstar's Q1 2026 average concert ticket is $108.63 — a 20% spread on that means $22 left on the table per ticket. On a four-pack, that's nearly $90.

**The fix:** Check three platforms minimum before clicking buy. Or skip the manual work — `ticketscan.io/compare` pulls all three at once.

---

### Sign 2 — You're shopping in the final 72 hours and the price hasn't dropped

☐ **The event is less than 72 hours out and the lowest available price is the same as it was last week.**

When resale prices haven't compressed by 72 hours pre-event, that's a signal the event is **demand-locked** — sellers won't drop further because they know the inventory will clear. Either the event is genuinely tight (in which case waiting hurts you), or the platform you're looking at is showing stale inventory (in which case look elsewhere).

**The fix:** Pull the same seat from a second platform. If the second platform is materially cheaper, the first is stale. If both match, the event is locked — buy now or skip.

---

### Sign 3 — You haven't set a target price before opening any platform

☐ **You opened a ticket platform without first deciding the maximum you'll pay.**

This is the silent overpay. The brain anchors to the first price it sees, then negotiates downward from there. The seat you eventually buy "feels like a deal" relative to the first listing, not relative to fair value. Behavioral pricing literature calls this "decoy anchoring" — every secondary platform exploits it.

**The fix:** Decide your floor before the browser tab opens. Write it down. If the lowest cross-platform price is above your floor, close the tab and try again in 48 hours.

---

### Sign 4 — The displayed price has no fees attached yet

☐ **You're looking at a price that says "from $XX" with no fee breakdown.**

In 2024, the FTC's Junk Fees Rule pushed every major platform to all-in displayed pricing. **If you're seeing pre-fee pricing in 2026, the platform is either non-compliant or you're on a regional version that hasn't updated.** The fee stack usually adds **22–35%** on resale and **18–28%** on primary. A "$95" ticket commonly checks out at $128.

**The fix:** Find the all-in total before comparing across platforms. Most platforms display it now; if not, force the cart to checkout and read the bottom line, then back out.

---

### Sign 5 — You have no price history for the event

☐ **You don't know whether the current price is high, low, or average for this event's history.**

Without history, every price feels reasonable. With history, you see the curve — most events drop 15–35% within 14 days of the show (concerts) or 6 hours of first pitch (sports). If you're buying at the peak of the curve and didn't know it, that's a 20–30% premium paid to information asymmetry.

**The fix:** Track the event for at least 7 days before buying (unless the event is less than 72 hours out — see Sign 2). TicketScan's watchlist logs the price every 4 hours and alerts you when it drops below your floor.
