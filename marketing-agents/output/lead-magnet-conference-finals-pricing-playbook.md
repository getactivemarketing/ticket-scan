**H1:** The two cheapest tickets in any playoff series

**Body (~250 words):**

If you are buying Conference Finals tickets in 2026, you have a problem most fans never solve: the same lower-bowl seat at the same arena can cost 4x more in Game 7 than in Game 1 of the same series. ### Page 3 — The pattern: how seven-game-series demand fragments

**H1:** Why Game 1 prices low

**Body (~300 words):**

Game 1 of any Conference Finals series is the strangest pricing moment of the playoffs. Both fan bases assume their team will win the series. Both fan bases plan to attend "the big one" — but they have not yet agreed on which game that is. Demand fragments across all seven potential games.

A fan who buys a single Game 1 ticket is committing to one game out of the seven. A fan who waits for Game 6 or Game 7 is committing to "the elimination game," and the elimination math is fully activated. The wait-for-Game-7 fan is bidding against every other wait-for-Game-7 fan in the market. The Game 1 fan is bidding only against the other Game 1 fans — a smaller pool, with a less compressed deadline.

The Game 1 floor effect is largest in the first 48 hours after the series matchup is set. After the second day, Game 1 prices begin to rise gradually as the schedule starts to firm up and broadcast partners start to promote specific game windows. By tip-off, Game 1 prices have typically risen 15-30% from their day-1 floor — still well below Games 6 and 7, but no longer at the structural low.

**H2:** Why Game 5 prices low

Game 5 is the orphan game in any series that goes 4-1. The "if-necessary" framing on Games 6 and 7 dominates the broadcast narrative. The schedule's mid-series moment is when fan attention is most divided — Game 4 just ended, Game 6 is still hypothetical, and Game 5 is the bridge. Demand softens.

In a series that goes 5 games or fewer, Game 5 is either the closeout game or it never happens at all. In a 5-game closeout, Game 5 prices firm up to Game 6-7 levels in the 24 hours after Game 4. But the cheap-Game-5 window holds through Game 4's start — buy before Game 4 tips off and the structural floor holds.

### Page 5 — The buying windows that move prices most

**H1:** When to buy each game type

**H2 + body for each window (~100 words each):**

**Game 1: Buy within 48 hours of the matchup being set.**
The structural floor holds for the first 48 hours after the prior round closes out. After hour 48, prices rise gradually as the schedule firms up and broadcast partners begin promoting. Tip-off is too late — by then, Game 1 prices are 15-30% above the day-1 floor. Set a price alert on TicketScan within 4 hours of the prior round ending, and the price alert fires inside the floor window for most series.

**Game 5: Buy before Game 4 tips off.**
The structural Game 5 floor holds through Game 4's start. The "is this the closeout game" tension activates 24 hours after Game 4 ends; if the series is 3-1 going into Game 4 and Game 4 closes it out, Game 5 never happens. If Game 4 extends the series to 2-2 or 3-1 with elimination implications on Game 5, the floor effect closes immediately. The cheapest window is during Game 3 or early Game 4 — counter-intuitive, because that is when most fans are focused on the current game, not the next one.

**Games 2, 3, 4: Default pricing, no structural lever.**
These are mid-series games with relatively flat pricing curves. Cross-platform variance is at its smallest. The buying playbook here is the standard cross-platform comparison playbook — check all four major platforms, pick the lowest fee-inclusive price, set a price alert for any 5%+ drop.

**Games 6 and 7: Wait for the day-of-game window.**
The Games 6-7 premium is fully activated 48 hours before tip-off. If you have not bought by then, the cheapest pricing is usually within the 6-hour window before tip-off — same-day-event resale liquidity fragments as fans give up on attending. Use the same-day playbook (link to the 4/28 blog post when it publishes Tue 5/19).

### Page 9 — Beyond Conference Finals: NBA Finals + Stanley Cup Finals + 2027 playoffs

**H1:** This playbook works for every seven-game series

**Body (~280 words):**

The Game 1 + Game 5 floor pattern is not unique to Conference Finals — it is structural to seven-game-series demand fragmentation. The same pattern applies to:

- **NBA Finals** (tipping off approximately June 5, 2026): Game 1 and Game 5 floor effect typically slightly larger than Conf Finals (-24% and -19% vs series median, based on 5 seasons of NBA Finals data). The Finals attract more national and casual attention, which compounds the structural Game 1 fragmentation.
- **Stanley Cup Finals** (tipping off approximately June 3, 2026): Game 1 and Game 5 floor effect similar to NHL Conf Finals (-20% and -16% vs series median). Smaller pure-fan demographics relative to NBA Finals means less compression, slightly flatter curves.
- **NBA Round 1 + Round 2 in 2027:** Round-1 series (best-of-7 since 2003) follow the same Game 1 + Game 5 pattern. The Round 1 floor effect is slightly smaller (-18% and -14%) because Round 1 attracts less casual demand. Round 2 sits between Round 1 and Conf Finals (-20% and -16%).
- **NHL Round 1 + Round 2 in 2027:** Same pattern as NBA but with slightly tighter spreads (smaller average series prices, less compression overall).
- **MLB LDS (5-game) + LCS (7-game):** The 5-game LDS modification: Game 1 holds the floor effect (-18% vs series median), Game 5 is the elimination game and prices accordingly (+45%). The LCS 7-game series follows the Conf Finals pattern. NBA + NHL fans buying playoff tickets across leagues can apply the same framework.

**Body callout (~50 words):**

> **The 3-step playbook scales to every seven-game series in your sports calendar.** Save this guide, bookmark `/compare` for each series, and the structural Game 1 + Game 5 floor effect will be working in your favor through Conf Finals 2026, NBA Finals 2026, Stanley Cup Finals 2026, and every 2027 playoff series.
