### Buy-window calendar strip (running below the tier table)

- **Mets vs Phillies:** May 30–June 1 (Queens) ●, July 22–24 (Philly) ○, August 28–30 (Queens) ●, September 25–27 (Philly) ○

### Section-band callout panel (right column, vertical stack)

Five callout boxes, one per section band, each with:

1. **BLEACHERS — visiting-fan-density tier.** Where premiums hit hardest on Yankees-Red-Sox and Cubs-Cardinals. Average non-rivalry: $25–$80. Rivalry: $60–$180. Math: visiting-team fans stack to be near their travel-mate cohort, and the resale-supply gets thin first because long-distance buyers don't release inventory near game-day.
2. **MAIN LEVEL CORNERS — institutional-buyer tier.** Standard premium (130–145%). Average non-rivalry: $80–$220. Rivalry: $180–$450. Math: corporate boxes + season-ticket-holder swap-outs damp the premium relative to general-public bleacher demand.
3. **FIELD LEVEL (Lower Bowl) — premium-experience tier.** Premium of 145–160% but on a high base. Average non-rivalry: $200–$650. Rivalry: $400–$1,300. Math: Field-level rivalry buyers are price-inelastic (the experience is the purchase).
4. **UPPER DECK / GRANDSTAND — budget-mainstream tier.** Premium of 110–125%. Average non-rivalry: $25–$80. Rivalry: $50–$160. Math: this is where the rivalry premium softens because the budget tier has the most elastic demand.
5. **LEGENDS / DIAMOND SUITES — premium-private tier.** Lowest premium of any tier (+35%). Average non-rivalry: $5,000–$20,000. Rivalry: $7,000–$25,000. Math: private-suite buyers are corporate; the premium is bounded by season-buyer contractual pricing.

### Bottom-of-page "Use this guide" block

Three-step prescription, numbered:

1. **Set a target price by tier.** Tier 1–3 rivalries: target the section average × 1.4. Tier 4–6 rivalries: target the section average × 1.2. Hit-rate ~30% within 14 days of the series for Tier 1–3, ~50% for Tier 4–6.
2. **Check three platforms minimum.** StubHub historically cheapest for Yankees + Red Sox; SeatGeek's Deal Score over-favors Bleachers because it doesn't penalize visiting-fan-density; Vivid Seats is the swing variable on rivalry pricing. **Use TicketScan to see all three at once.**
3. **Buy 36 hours before first pitch — not earlier, not later.** Rivalry-week pricing follows a non-monotonic curve. Day-of-game drops are smaller than mid-week-of pricing dips because supply tightens as the series progresses.

## Design brief for the design team

**Hierarchy (top to bottom):**

1. Headline block (largest type)
2. Tier table (the hero — should take ~50% of vertical real estate)
3. Buy-window calendar strip (horizontal band, ~15% of vertical)
4. Section-band callout panel (vertical stack, runs alongside / below the tier table — ~25% of vertical)
5. "Use this guide" three-step block (~8% of vertical)
6. Footer line + CTA + logo (~2% of vertical)

**Visual texture:**

- The tier table is the visual anchor. Use clear horizontal rules between rows, NO vertical rules (vertical rules clutter at infographic scale). Tier-1 row gets a colored band underlay (orange tint, 10% opacity) to signal "highest premium" without overwhelming. Tier 6 gets a green tint at the same opacity to signal "lightest premium."
- The buy-window calendar strip should read as a single visual unit, not five separate strips. Use a single date axis with five row-tracks; circles ●/○ on each track keyed to color.
- The section-band callouts should NOT use percentage circles or gauges — those are infographic clichés. Use a simple two-line callout: top line is the section name + ALL-CAPS premium-band label; second line is the dollar-range delta.

**What to avoid:**

- No baseball-stadium illustration as decoration (cliché). The data IS the visual.
- No "compare ticket prices" stock imagery. The infographic stands on the data alone.
- No three-dimensional graphs, no pie charts (premium percentages don't sum to 100%, so a pie chart is wrong on first principles).
- No QR code (the URL at the bottom is enough; QR codes hurt scannability of share-screenshots).

**Accessibility:**

- Color-blind-safe palette confirmed: orange `#F97316` vs green `#10B981` is distinguishable in red-green color blindness; the orange→green spectrum maps to a brightness gradient that reads as "high premium → low premium" even in monochrome.
- Body text minimum 12pt at PDF size, equivalent at PNG.
- Logo and footer URL must be readable when the asset is rendered at 600px-wide (Twitter inline image preview).
