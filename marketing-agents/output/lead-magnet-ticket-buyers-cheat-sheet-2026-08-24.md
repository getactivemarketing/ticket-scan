## Page 1 — The five-minute pre-check

Before opening a ticket marketplace, write down:

1. **Event:** exact artist, team, show, or session.
2. **Date flexibility:** exact date, nearby dates, or any date in a range.
3. **Seat requirement:** section/tier, view requirement, accessible seating, and quantity.
4. **All-in ceiling:** the maximum total you will pay, including fees and delivery.
5. **Buy deadline:** the last date you are comfortable waiting until.

> **Rule:** If you do not set a ceiling before browsing, the first listing you see becomes your anchor.

## Page 2 — When to check by event type

| Event type | First check | Useful recheck window | What changes the advice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stadium concert | 6–8 weeks before show | 10–14 days and final 72 hours | Artist demand, production holds, venue size, and seat tier |
| Arena concert | 4–8 weeks before show | 7–10 days | Tour routing, weekday vs weekend, and inventory depth |
| NFL / major sports | As soon as schedule and inventory are known | 2–4 weeks, then the final week | Opponent, standings, weather, rivalry, and resale supply |
| MLB / regular-season baseball | Compare several dates early | 3–10 days | Day of week, promotions, weather, and team performance |
| Theater / Broadway | At announcement or on-sale | Weekly; sooner for limited runs | Capacity, reviews, cast, and sell-through |
| Festival | At announcement or next price tier | Before the tier deadline | Lineup, tier changes, and thin resale supply |
| Tennis session event | Compare session types immediately | Weekly, then before the desired round | Day/night session, court access, player draw, and round |

These are planning rules, not guarantees. Prices can rise or fall at any point.

## Page 3 — Compare like with like

Use this checklist for every candidate listing:

☐ Same event and date  
☐ Same session or game time  
☐ Same seat section or equivalent tier  
☐ Same ticket quantity  
☐ Same delivery method and delivery timing  
☐ Fees shown or clearly marked as unknown  
☐ Total price recorded, not just the headline price  
☐ Checked-at timestamp recorded  

> A $120 listing is not a deal comparison if the other listing is a different section, quantity, delivery method, or fee treatment.

## Page 4 — The buy / wait / hold decision

### BUY

- The all-in total is at or below your ceiling.
- The event is close and the seat meets your non-negotiables.
- You have compared equivalent options and verified delivery.

### WAIT

- Listings are not comparable yet.
- The gap between sources is large enough to justify another check.
- You have date or seat flexibility and are outside the event’s final window.

### HOLD

- The current option fits your range, but the event is not urgent.
- You have not completed at least two time-stamped checks.
- You need more information about fees, delivery, or the seat view.

No rule guarantees the lowest price. The point is to make the decision deliberate.
