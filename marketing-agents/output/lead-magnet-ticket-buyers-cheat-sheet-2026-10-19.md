# Lead Magnet: The Ticket Buyer’s Cheat Sheet

**Suggested file name:** `ticket-buyer-cheat-sheet.pdf`  
**Audience:** People preparing to buy concert, sports, theater, or live-event tickets  
**Primary goal:** Earn an email signup by giving readers a short, printable pre-checkout routine.  
**Secondary CTA:** Find the event and where to buy it at TicketScan; optionally save the event for later.

## Cover

### The Ticket Buyer’s Cheat Sheet

### 7 checks to make before you pay for a ticket

Use this one-page routine whenever a listing looks urgent, unusually cheap, or confusing.

---

## Page 1 — The seven checks

### 1. Confirm the event identity

- Is this the right artist, team, show, or matchup?
- Is it the right city and venue?
- Is the date and start time correct, including the time zone?
- Is this the event itself—not a parking pass, merchandise item, VIP package, or waitlist?

### 2. Read the onsale or presale rules

- Is this a presale, general onsale, resale listing, or waitlist?
- Do you need a code, membership, card, or account?
- Is there a ticket limit?
- Does the event page list a local start time?

### 3. Compare the same seat, not just the same event

When checking more than one listing, match as many details as possible:

- section, row, and seat range;
- view or obstruction notes;
- standing-room, general-admission, or reserved seating status;
- ticket quantity and whether seats are together.

“Same event” does not mean “same ticket.”

### 4. Inspect the venue map

Check the venue’s section names, entrances, sightline notes, and accessibility information. A lower-looking section number or a cheaper-looking card is not automatically a better view.

### 5. Find the real seller and delivery method

Before paying, identify who is fulfilling the order and when the ticket will arrive.

- mobile transfer, mobile barcode, print-at-home, or another method;
- instant delivery versus delivery closer to the event;
- resale restrictions or transfer limitations;
- refund or replacement terms if delivery fails.

### 6. Expand the final checkout total

The first number on a listing is not necessarily the amount charged. Before entering payment details, check:

- service and facility fees;
- delivery fees;
- taxes;
- currency;
- the final total for the exact quantity of tickets.

### 7. Save the details you may need later

Keep the confirmation, order number, delivery instructions, venue address, and support contact. If you are still deciding, save the event in your TicketScan watchlist so you can return to the event details without starting over.

---

## Page 2 — Five red flags

Pause and verify if:

1. the page does not clearly identify the venue or date;
2. the listing title sounds like the artist but the item type says parking, VIP, or merchandise;
3. seat details are missing or “limited view” is unexplained;
4. the delivery promise is vague or arrives after the event;
5. the checkout total changes in a way you cannot explain.

Urgency is not a substitute for readable terms.

## Final call to action

**Find the event first. Read the venue details. Then check the listing.**

TicketScan helps you find events, onsale details, venue guides, and places to buy. It does not replace the seller’s checkout terms—always confirm the final details before paying.

## Design and production spec

- **Format:** two-page portrait PDF, US Letter and A4 variants.
- **Visual style:** high-contrast black, white, and ticket-stub red; large numbered checks; generous writing space.
- **Accessibility:** selectable text, tagged headings, minimum 4.5:1 body-text contrast, no information conveyed by color alone.
- **Placement:** checklist blog post, newsletter signup confirmation, exit-intent popup, and a dedicated `/guides/ticket-buyer-cheat-sheet` landing page.
- **Form fields:** email address and optional event category; do not require a target price or imply price alerts.
- **Email handoff:** send immediately after confirmed signup; follow with a day-3 email about reading venue maps and checkout totals.
