### A two-minute check before you click “Buy”

Ticket prices move quickly, but urgency does not make every price a good price. Use this checklist to slow down, compare the right details, and decide from a number you chose in advance.

**Keep this rule:** Never compare two different seats and call it a price comparison.

## The checklist

### [ ] 1. You are comparing listings, not the same ticket

Two listings can look similar while having different sections, rows, delivery terms, view quality, or ticket quantities. A $90 upper-level seat is not automatically a better deal than a $115 lower-level seat.

**Check:** event, date, section, row or seat area, quantity, delivery, and seller/platform.

**Do this:** Compare the same event and the closest equivalent seat before judging the price.

### [ ] 2. You are reacting to a countdown or “last chance” message

Timers and low-inventory messages can be useful, but they are not proof that the current listing is your best option. Panic removes your ability to compare.

**Check:** Can you name your maximum all-in budget? Have you checked at least two marketplaces?

**Do this:** Set a price ceiling. If the current ticket is above it, save the event and check again later when your plan allows.

### [ ] 3. You are looking only at the advertised price

The listing price may not be the amount charged at checkout. Fees, delivery, taxes, and marketplace-specific disclosures can change the total.

**Check:** Is the displayed amount an all-in total or an initial listing price?

**Do this:** Compare the clearest available total, record what is included, and verify the final checkout amount before paying.

### [ ] 4. You checked one marketplace and assumed it won

There is no permanent “cheapest site” for every event, seat, and moment. Inventory and seller pricing vary.

**Check:** Did you compare Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, and StubHub—or record why one source was unavailable?

**Do this:** Use TicketScan to compare the event side by side, then verify the final purchase details on the ticket provider.

### [ ] 5. You have no target price or walk-away point

Without a target, every price can feel urgent. A target turns a vague hope for a deal into a decision rule.

**Check:** What is your maximum total budget? What seat quality is acceptable? How long can you wait?

**Do this:** Add the event to your TicketScan watchlist and set a target price when that feature is available. If the price hits your rule, decide; if it does not, walk away or revise the plan deliberately.
