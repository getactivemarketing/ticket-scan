**Implementation:**

2. **`web/src/app/blog/[slug]/page.tsx`** — 
   - Imported `DEFAULT_BLOG_IMAGE` and computed `articleImage = post.image || DEFAULT_BLOG_IMAGE`.
   - Converted standalone `Article` JSON-LD into a `@graph` container holding `Article` + `BreadcrumbList`, each with stable `@id`.
   - Added `image`, `keywords`, `articleSection` properties on Article.
   - Upgraded `publisher` to include nested `logo` as `ImageObject` (Google's recommended structure).

**Why a fallback image instead of per-post images:**
- Generating 21 unique 1200×630 OG images is design work; today's task budget is 30 minutes.
- An optional `image` field on `BlogPost` means individual posts can override the fallback as branded images get produced — the schema and OG tags will pick up the per-post URL automatically.
- Google's Article rich-result eligibility check requires *an* image to be present; the 512×512 logo passes validation and unblocks site-wide eligibility today, while leaving room for a proper per-post upgrade later.

## Competitor Insight

Spot-checked **SeatGeek's blog** (`seatgeek.com/blog/`):

- Their blog post pages emit `BlogPosting` (not `Article`) — both are valid; `BlogPosting` is a subtype of `Article` and slightly more semantically specific. Worth considering a swap on our side; it's a one-token change with no downside.
- Their Article schema includes `wordCount` — Google doesn't require it but uses it as a quality signal. We track `readTime`; could derive `wordCount` from `content.length / 5` (rough words/char) at build time.
- They include a `Person` author with `url` linking to author bio pages. We use `Organization` (Ticket Scan Team), which is technically valid but Google's docs prefer `Person` when an individual byline applies. Currently all our posts share one byline so the `Organization` author is appropriate — flag for future when posts gain individual authors.

Easy wins from this audit: `BlogPosting` swap + `wordCount`. Both can ride along on the next blog edit.
