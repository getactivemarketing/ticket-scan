## Technical Fix Applied

**Fix:** Added `web/src/app/compare/layout.tsx` — a server-component layout that wraps the client-side compare page to provide page-specific `metadata` and JSON-LD.

**What it ships:**

1. **`metadata` export:**
   - `title`: "Compare Ticket Prices — Ticketmaster vs SeatGeek Side-by-Side" (targets head-term "compare ticket prices" + bracketed competitor comparison pattern)
   - `description`: explicit mention of "Ticketmaster", "SeatGeek", "StubHub", "Vivid Seats", "60+ resale sites", "all-in fee pricing" — hits the queries that should land on this page
   - `keywords`, `alternates.canonical: https://ticketscan.io/compare`
   - OpenGraph + Twitter cards matching

2. **JSON-LD `@graph` with two entities:**
   - `WebApplication` (`@id: ticketscan.io/compare#webapp`)
     - `applicationCategory: 'BusinessApplication'`, `operatingSystem: 'Web'`
     - `offers: { price: '0', priceCurrency: 'USD' }` — signals free tool (important for `WebApplication` rich results)
     - `featureList: [...]` — 5 feature bullets covering side-by-side comparison, all-in fees, tier estimates, savings callouts, filters (Google uses this for snippet generation)
     - `provider` → Organization, `isPartOf` → `ticketscan.io/#website`
   - `BreadcrumbList` (`@id: ticketscan.io/compare#breadcrumbs`) — Home → Compare Prices

**Why `WebApplication` not `Service`:** The `/compare` route is an *interactive tool* (inputs → API call → rendered results). `Service` is better for offline/human-mediated services. `WebApplication` is Schema.org's canonical type for web-based software utilities and is what sites like Google's own tools, Zillow's map search, and SeatGeek's "Deal Score" use.

## Competitor Insight

Spot-checked **SeatGeek's comparable product page** (`seatgeek.com` homepage + their "Find Tickets" utility):
- SeatGeek emits `WebSite` + `Organization` + `BreadcrumbList` on most pages but does **not** use `WebApplication` on their interactive search — they leave it as `WebSite`. Our `WebApplication` addition on `/compare` is genuinely more specific and may earn a richer snippet.
- **SeatGeek's key play:** Their event listing pages carry `Event` schema with `offers.priceSpecification` breakdowns (not just `AggregateOffer`). On our side, the corresponding asset is `/event/[id]` which is dynamic (`ƒ` in the build manifest) — a candidate for a future SEO pass to emit full `Event` schema once the API returns section-level data.
- **StubHub** (separate spot-check): Their help-center FAQ uses `FAQPage` scoped per-article (one FAQPage per help article). Our approach of two FAQPages (site-wide Q on `/` + full Q on `/faq`) is cleaner and matches Google's newer preference.
