# SEO requests — 2026-10-22

## Remove retired product claims from legacy blog posts

The deployed blog index and multiple posts still contain claims that TicketScan tracks prices, sends price-drop alerts, or issues buy/wait recommendations. Those claims are currently false and must be rewritten before further promotion or internal linking.

Examples found in `web/src/data/blog.ts`: “Set a price alert,” “our data shows,” “track prices,” “we’ll notify you,” “Track Price Trends,” and “Our buy recommendation.”

Keep only advice that is general and clearly not TicketScan data: compare platforms, inspect the all-in checkout total, verify the event/date/section, and use official onsale or presale information.

## Safe keyword opportunities

- `[venue] seating guide and sections`
- `[venue] capacity and parking`
- `[city] concert tickets onsale dates`
- `[city] NFL/NBA/NHL/MLB tickets`
- `World Cup 2026 stadium guides`
- `how to compare ticket fees and all-in prices`
- `how to check ticket presale dates`

Avoid targeting “ticket price history,” “price-drop alerts,” “best time to buy” as a TicketScan capability, or any statistic attributed to TicketScan price data until the integrations are restored.
