## Thread copy

**1/8**

Ticket prices are not comparable just because they are both called “Section 200.”

Before you call one listing cheaper, run the same four checks. A five-minute comparison can save you from a very expensive plot twist.

**2/8**

**Check 1: same event.**

Same artist or team. Same date. Same venue. Same session.

“The show” can have multiple dates, sessions, or nearby venues. Match the event before you match the price.

**3/8**

**Check 2: equivalent seats.**

Match section, row quality, view, ticket quantity, and any obstructed-view or accessibility note.

A $20 difference is not a deal if the inventory is not equivalent.

**4/8**

**Check 3: the number you will actually pay.**

Look for fees, delivery, taxes, and required add-ons. The headline listing is an opening sentence, not the conclusion.

Compare all-in totals when they are available—and verify at checkout.

**5/8**

**Check 4: time and delivery.**

Record when you checked. Prices and inventory move.

Then read the delivery method, transfer timing, and venue restrictions. A ticket that arrives too late is not a bargain.

**6/8**

Now pick your ceiling.

Write down the maximum you are comfortable paying before you scroll. Otherwise, a countdown timer and a “low inventory” label get to negotiate on your behalf.

They are terrible negotiators.

**7/8**

TicketScan’s useful job is the second-screen check: discover an event, compare available sources, and make the decision with the same event and equivalent inventory in view.

No single marketplace wins every event. That is the point.

**8/8**

The anti-overpay routine:

1. Match the event.
2. Match the seat.
3. Check the total.
4. Set your ceiling.
5. Verify delivery.

Compare before you buy → ticketscan.io/compare
