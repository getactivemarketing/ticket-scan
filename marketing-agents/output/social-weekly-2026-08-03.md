### Patterns and decisions

- **Specific beats generic.** A real event or a clearly labeled price example gives the audience a reason to stop.
- **All-in pricing is the strongest repeatable click angle.** Keep using concrete examples, but label the source and timestamp and do not generalize one listing into a market-wide claim.
- **Consumer-control language works.** “Set a ceiling,” “compare first,” and “walk away” are distinctive and safer than unsupported “cheapest” claims.
- **Same-day content is useful only when verified.** Keep a verification gate for event date, venue, and listing freshness.
- **No viral or near-viral moment can be confirmed.** Platform analytics are missing; treat the proxy winners as candidates for measurement and paid testing, not as proven viral posts.

**Measurement request:** add post ID, platform, publish timestamp, impressions/reach, engagements, link clicks, follows, saves/shares, and UTM campaign to the next social export. Until then, preserve the unmeasured label in weekly reports.

## 4. Competitor social intelligence

This is public-positioning intelligence, not engagement measurement; competitor post-level analytics were not available.

### SeatGeek

SeatGeek’s current August event content leans on marketplace convenience, interactive seat maps, Deal Score, price filters, buyer protection, price alerts, and last-minute flexibility. Its positioning is “find the best deal inside SeatGeek.”

**Differentiation opportunity:** TicketScan should own the “same event across multiple marketplaces” frame. Use equivalent-seat examples and all-in totals, while avoiding a claim that any one source is always cheapest. SeatGeek’s own August guide is a useful reminder that price timing is conditional, not universal. Source: https://seatgeek.com/august-tickets

### StubHub

Public LinkedIn positioning remains partnership- and event-led, with World Cup distribution and major live-event relationships foregrounded. The tone is broad reach and access rather than buyer education.

**Differentiation opportunity:** translate major-event scale into practical buyer rules: total price, seat equivalence, transfer constraints, and when waiting is rational. Do not mirror FOMO-heavy “buy now” language without a verified market signal. Source: https://www.linkedin.com/company/stubhub

### Ticketmaster

Ticketmaster’s structural advantage is official onsale and primary-inventory access. Its promotional posture is likely to cluster around onsales, artist/team announcements, and official event information rather than cross-market comparison.

**Differentiation opportunity:** position TicketScan as the neutral second screen after discovery: check the official listing, then compare the same event and equivalent seat across available sources. Never imply resale is always better or official inventory is always worse.

### Competitive response plan

- Publish the checklist carousel as a saveable consumer utility asset.
- Make “same event, equivalent seat, all-in total” the recurring visual grammar.
- Counter FOMO with a ceiling-first rule and conditional timing advice.
- Use major-event posts to teach a buying behavior, not merely repeat that tickets are available.
