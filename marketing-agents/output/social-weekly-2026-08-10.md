### Top three by engagement potential

These are editorial proxies from last week’s shipped/planned content, not platform winners.

1. **“The countdown clock is not your financial advisor.”** Short, irreverent, broadly relatable, and strongly aligned with the consumer-advocate voice.
2. **J. Cole same-day buying rule.** Event specificity plus a clear price-ceiling behavior gives the audience a practical action and a reason to reply or save.
3. **“Free” event costs.** The counterintuitive total-outing-cost angle expands the conversation beyond the listing price and invites personal examples.

### Top three by click intent

1. **$30.07 vs. $38.19 estimated-total example.** Concrete, timestamped, and directly connected to the compare CTA. Verify freshness before reuse.
2. **Platform-gap post.** Explains the product’s core value: one marketplace is only one sample of the market.
3. **NFL preseason window.** Timely sports intent with a natural bridge to the week’s NFL buying-guide refresh.

### Bottom three / performance-risk queue

1. **Generic platform-gap variant.** The point is useful but abstract without an event, section, timestamp, or visual proof.
2. **Free-event explainer on Threads.** More explanatory than conversational; compress into a question or quote-ready observation.
3. **“Quieter preseason window” framing.** Cautious and non-specific; add a verified event or make it a simple checklist instead.

### Patterns and decisions

- **Specific beats generic:** event, section, timestamp, and a clear comparison rule improve the stopping power of the idea.
- **All-in price is the strongest repeatable click angle:** label examples as estimates or observations and never generalize a single listing into a market-wide claim.
- **Consumer-control language is distinctive:** “set a ceiling,” “match the seat,” and “walk away” are safer and more ownable than “cheapest.”
- **Questions should expose a tradeoff:** weekend vs. weekday, view vs. total, certainty vs. flexibility.
- **World Cup content has changed jobs:** the tournament ended July 19. Use it only as an evergreen post-event lesson; no active countdown or inventory urgency.
- **No viral or near-viral moment is verifiable** without platform exports.

Its August event hub reinforces convenience and comparison *within* SeatGeek, while its summer concert report provides a data-led editorial hook. ### StubHub

The current public newsroom emphasis is event and partnership-led. The latest surfaced item is a July 2026 partnership announcement, not a clear consumer pricing campaign. Source: [StubHub newsroom](https://newsroom.stubhub.com/).

**Opportunity:** translate event scale into practical buyer education: delivery, transfer restrictions, equivalent seats, and total price. Counter urgency with a ceiling-first rule.

### Ticketmaster

Ticketmaster remains the official ticketing partner for the 2026 US Open and is structurally strongest around primary onsales and official event information. Source: [Ticketmaster US Open page](https://www.ticketmaster.com/usopentennis?ac_link=schemalink).

**Opportunity:** position TicketScan as the neutral second screen: check official inventory, then compare equivalent inventory across available sources. Do not frame official inventory as inherently worse.
