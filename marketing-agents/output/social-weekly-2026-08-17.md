- Product snapshots should be labeled as TicketScan samples, not market-wide statistics.
- Price history is stale after July 24 in the latest local snapshots. Do not call it a current deal or promise an alert based on it.
- No viral or near-viral moment is verifiable without platform exports.

## 3. Competitor social intelligence

This is public positioning intelligence, not post-level engagement measurement.

### SeatGeek

SeatGeek’s current August hub leads with event discovery, a large marketplace, buyer protection, and comparison language within its own inventory. Its public event pages also emphasize fluctuating prices and interactive seat maps. Sources: [August tickets](https://seatgeek.com/august-tickets), [SeatGeek newsroom](https://seatgeek.com/enterprise/newsroom).

**Opportunity:** own the neutral second-screen position: same event, equivalent seat, same total, multiple sources. Explain the comparison method rather than claiming a permanent price advantage.

### StubHub

Search surfaced current public conversation around event-led social and a recent deleted TikTok controversy, but no stable, verifiable August consumer campaign worth mirroring. Treat social chatter as a monitoring signal, not a fact pattern. Source: [public discussion surfaced in search](https://www.reddit.com/r/Fauxmoi/comments/1vnoecl/stub_hub_posts_and_deletes_tiktok_shading_bts_for/).

**Opportunity:** lead with practical trust content: delivery timing, transfer restrictions, fees, and what “protected” does and does not mean. Keep the tone factual and avoid pile-ons.

### Ticketmaster

Ticketmaster’s current public strength remains official event inventory and onsale information; its US Open presence is especially relevant this week. Use the [Ticketmaster US Open page](https://www.ticketmaster.com/usopentennis) as the primary-inventory reference.

**Opportunity:** position TicketScan as the neutral comparison layer: check official inventory first, then compare equivalent available listings. Do not imply official inventory is inherently worse.

### Differentiation and response plan

- Repeat the visual grammar: **same event / same seat / same total / timestamp**.
- Counter FOMO with a ceiling-first rule and conditional language.
- Use the US Open as a session-choice series, not a generic availability push.
- Use competitor news as a prompt for buyer education, never as an unsupported attack.
